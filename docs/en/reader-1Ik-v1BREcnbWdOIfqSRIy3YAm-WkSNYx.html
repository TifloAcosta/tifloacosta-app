General introduction

WhatsApp has become one of those tools we no longer use just for conversation. It is used to receive documents, send voice messages, coordinate groups, ask for help, work, share links, confirm appointments, send photos, manage communities, and deal with matters that once belonged to phone calls, email, or even paper. Not always for the better, but there it is, installed in daily life with the quiet persistence of a leak you keep meaning to fix.

Many blind users already handle WhatsApp on the iPhone with reasonable confidence. However, when you move to the Mac, the situation changes. The screen is different, the app is organized differently, the keyboard becomes more important, and VoiceOver requires a way of moving around that is not always obvious at first.

This course exists to cover precisely that gap: learning how to use WhatsApp on the Mac with VoiceOver in a practical, orderly, and understandable way. It is not about explaining WhatsApp as if nobody had ever sent a message before. It is also not about turning every step into a technical novel. The idea is simpler: to guide the user from the first contact with WhatsApp for Mac toward reasonably independent use of the application.

Throughout the course, you will learn how to install the application, link it with the iPhone, understand the main window, move between chats, read messages, write, send files, record voice messages, review notifications, handle groups, and solve common problems.

The course is designed especially for blind users who work with VoiceOver on the Mac. That is why it is not enough to say “click here” or “look for this button.” We need to explain how to get there, how to recognize the correct area, how to know whether focus is where it should be, and what to do when the application does not respond as expected.

Keep one idea in mind from the beginning: WhatsApp for Mac can be a very useful tool, but it does not always behave as cleanly as one might wish. Some areas may change name, some controls may vary depending on the version, and some tasks may still be easier on the iPhone than on the Mac. That does not mean the app cannot be used. It means the user must learn how to orient themselves inside the application instead of relying only on a memorized recipe.

A VoiceOver user does not need to repeat steps like an obedient robot. They need to understand the logic of the window, recover focus, recognize the message field, find the messages, and distinguish whether they are in the chat list or inside a conversation. That will be one of the main goals of this course.

This document is not intended to replace a full VoiceOver for Mac course. Some basic VoiceOver commands will be necessary, and they will be explained when needed. But the center of the learning process is WhatsApp, not all of macOS.

The final goal is for the user to be able to open WhatsApp on the Mac, read conversations, reply safely, send content, manage chats, and solve the most frequent situations without constantly depending on the iPhone.

This is not about proving that the Mac is better than the iPhone, or that using WhatsApp on the computer is essential. It is about adding one more tool. For some tasks, the iPhone will still be faster. For others, the Mac will be more comfortable, especially when writing long messages, managing documents, reviewing long conversations, or working with several resources at once.

By the end of this course, the user should not feel they have read a scattered collection of instructions. They should have a clear idea of how WhatsApp behaves on the Mac and how it can be managed with VoiceOver in real situations.

Learning a tool does not mean knowing where every button is on a perfect afternoon. It means being able to use it when a message arrives, when there is a hurry, when focus goes where it should not, when the app changes a detail, when a voice message fails to record, when a group becomes a storm, or when you need to send a document without staging a small technological ceremony. That is the purpose of this course.

Module 0. Basic concepts and requirements

Before installing anything, it is worth understanding exactly what you are going to use. Many difficulties appear because people begin at the end: they open the app, try to scan a code, press keys without knowing where focus is, and five minutes later suspect the Mac has developed a personal grudge against them.

The reality is usually less dramatic. WhatsApp for Mac works as an extension of the WhatsApp account already used on the phone. You do not create a new account. You do not install an independent WhatsApp account. You do not start from scratch. You link the Mac to the account already active on the iPhone.

What WhatsApp for Mac is

WhatsApp for Mac is the official WhatsApp application designed to use your account from a Mac computer. It lets you read and send messages, participate in groups, share files, receive notifications, send voice messages, and use other common WhatsApp functions from the computer. The account is still the same one used on the iPhone. Your chats, contacts, and groups belong to your WhatsApp account, not to the Mac. The Mac acts as a linked device.

What a linked device means

A linked device is a computer or device authorized to use your WhatsApp account. In this case, the Mac is linked to the account you already have active on the iPhone.

To link it, WhatsApp displays a QR code on the Mac. That code is scanned from the iPhone, inside WhatsApp, in the Linked Devices section. Once the process is complete, the Mac is authorized to access your conversations.

You do not type the QR code, copy it, or dictate it. You scan it with the iPhone camera from inside the WhatsApp app.

What you need to follow this course

To follow this course, it is useful to have the following ready:

  1. A Mac with a recent version of macOS.
  2. VoiceOver turned on and working correctly.
  3. WhatsApp installed and working on the iPhone.
  4. An Internet connection on the Mac.
  5. An Internet connection on the iPhone during the linking process.
  6. Basic knowledge of the Mac keyboard.
  7. The ability to move with VoiceOver through windows, buttons, and text fields.

You do not need to be a Mac expert. If you were, you probably would not be reading this module. But you should know the basics: moving with VoiceOver, activating buttons, typing text, opening applications, and switching between elements in a window.

What you should know about VoiceOver before starting

VoiceOver uses a key combination called the VO command. It is usually Control plus Option. Caps Lock can also be configured as the VO key. When this course says “VO command” or “VO plus,” it refers to that combination. For example, when the course says “VO plus Right Arrow,” it means Control plus Option plus Right Arrow, unless the user has configured another VO key.

It will also be important to understand the concept of interacting. In many areas of macOS, VoiceOver does not automatically enter all the internal elements. First you interact with an area, list, table, or group. When finished, you stop interacting to return to the previous level. This is one of the major differences compared with Windows, where users often move with Tab, arrow keys, focus mode, or browse mode.

Difference between using WhatsApp on iPhone and on Mac

On the iPhone, everything is handled on a touch screen. VoiceOver lets you explore with your finger, move with gestures, activate items with a double tap, and dictate messages very directly.

On the Mac, the workflow is mainly keyboard based. The application appears inside a window with several areas: chat list, active conversation, message field, toolbar, and different buttons. The user must learn how to move through that structure.

The main advantage is that writing long text is usually more comfortable. It may also be easier to manage files, copy text, review long conversations, or work while other windows are open. The disadvantage is that initial orientation may be less immediate: on the iPhone, the finger helps explore the screen; on the Mac, everything depends on focus and knowing which area you are in.

Which version of WhatsApp to use

The recommended option is to use the official WhatsApp application for Mac. It can be obtained from the official WhatsApp website or from the App Store, depending on availability at the time. Do not install unofficial versions, modified apps, or third party programs promising special features. WhatsApp contains personal conversations, files, contacts, private data, and often sensitive information. It is not the place to experiment with questionable software, no matter how confidently the Internet invites you to click Accept like it is a competitive sport.

WhatsApp Web can also be used from a browser, but this course focuses on the desktop application for Mac.

What not to do

  1. Do not install WhatsApp from unknown websites.
  2. Do not try to create a new account if you already have WhatsApp on the iPhone.
  3. Do not share verification codes with anyone.
  4. Do not link the Mac on a computer you do not trust.
  5. Do not leave sessions open on shared computers.
  6. Do not press random keys when VoiceOver does not read what you expect.
  7. Do not assume every problem is the user’s fault.

That last point matters. If something is not clear at first, it may be due to the interface, focus, a version change, a missing permission, or an area that requires interaction. The solution is not self-blame. The solution is to regain orientation.

  2. Confirm that you can send and receive messages.
  3. Go to the Settings tab in WhatsApp on the iPhone.
  4. Find the Linked Devices option.
  5. Confirm that you know how to turn on VoiceOver on the Mac.
  6. Open any application on the Mac and practice moving with VO plus Right Arrow and VO plus Left Arrow.
  7. Practice interacting with an area and stopping interaction.

By the end of this module, you should understand what WhatsApp for Mac is, how it relates to the iPhone, what it means to link a device, and which basic VoiceOver skills you need to continue.

Module 1. Installing WhatsApp on the Mac

Before using WhatsApp on the Mac, you need to install the application. This step sounds simple, and normally it is, as long as you do not begin by downloading whatever appears first in a search engine. When private messaging is involved, it is worth being slightly more cautious than a squirrel crossing a motorway.

Installation should be done from a reliable source. WhatsApp contains personal conversations, files, phone numbers, groups, and private data. You do not want look-alike apps, copies, or programs advertised as “enhanced” versions.

Where to get WhatsApp for Mac

The recommended way to install WhatsApp for Mac is to use an official source: the WhatsApp website, in its download section, or the App Store if the official app is available for your version of macOS. If using the App Store, verify that the developer is the correct one and that it is not a third-party application.

Installing from the App Store

  1. Open the App Store on the Mac, from the Dock, Launchpad, Spotlight, or the Applications folder.
  2. Move to the search field and type WhatsApp.
  3. Make sure it is the official application. Do not install apps with similar names, strange additions, or miracle utilities. Technological miracles usually come with fine print and excessive permissions.
  4. Choose Get or Install. The Mac may ask for your Apple ID password, Touch ID, or confirmation.
  5. Wait for the download to finish. When it is complete, the app will be installed and you can open it from the App Store, Launchpad, or the Applications folder.

Installing from the official website

  1. Open Safari or another browser on the Mac.
  2. Go to the official WhatsApp website and find the download section.
  3. Choose the Mac download and download the file.
  4. Open the downloaded file and follow the installation instructions. You may need to drag WhatsApp to the Applications folder, which is common on macOS.

Opening WhatsApp for the first time

Once the application is installed, open it. You can use Launchpad, the Applications folder, or Spotlight. With Spotlight, press Command plus Space, type WhatsApp, and press Enter when VoiceOver announces the application.

The first time it opens, macOS may show a warning saying the application was downloaded from the Internet or needs confirmation before opening. If it comes from an official source, you can accept.

What appears the first time

When WhatsApp opens for the first time on the Mac, it usually shows a linking screen with a QR code. VoiceOver may announce this area in different ways: it may read text about linking, say there is an image, announce a code, or land on a button in the window. It is not a problem if the QR code is not understood as text. It is an image that must be scanned with the iPhone; it is not something to read or type.

Initial permissions

During use, macOS may ask WhatsApp for permission to use certain features. These permissions do not all appear during installation; some appear the first time you try to use a specific function:

  1. Microphone, for recording voice messages or making calls.
  2. Camera, for video calls.
  3. Notifications, for message alerts.
  4. Contacts, if the application needs access to them.
  5. Files and folders, if documents will be sent or saved.

It is recommended to grant the permissions needed for the functions you actually plan to use. If you do not use video calls, you do not need to rush with the camera. If you will send voice messages, the microphone is essential.

If WhatsApp does not open

  1. Make sure the Mac has Internet access.
  2. Check that installation finished correctly.
  3. Look for WhatsApp in the Applications folder.
  4. Close the app if it is open but not responding, and open it again.
  5. Restart the Mac if the problem continues.
  6. Check whether your version of macOS is compatible with the installed WhatsApp version.

If VoiceOver does not read the first window clearly

If VoiceOver does not read the first WhatsApp screen clearly, do not start pressing keys at random:

  1. Press VO plus Right Arrow to move through elements.
  2. Press VO plus Left Arrow to go back.
  3. Press Tab to check for interactive controls.
  4. Use VO plus F2 to review open windows if needed.
  5. Close and reopen WhatsApp if VoiceOver seems to have lost focus.

At first launch, the most important thing is to reach the screen where the QR code appears. Once the code is visible, the next step is done from the iPhone.

  1. Install WhatsApp for Mac from an official source.

  3. Confirm that the linking screen appears.
  4. Explore the window with VoiceOver.
  5. Find any text related to linking the device.

By the end of this module, you should have WhatsApp installed on the Mac and open on the linking screen.

Module 2. Linking WhatsApp on the iPhone with the Mac

Linking is the step that lets you use your WhatsApp account on the Mac. It is simple once understood, but it can be awkward for a blind user because two devices are involved at the same time: the Mac displays a QR code and the iPhone uses the camera to scan it. This is the kind of setup where camera, screen reader, QR code, and another open window all decide to join the same little circus, so it is worth going step by step.

General linking process

  1. Open WhatsApp on the Mac and leave the QR code visible.
  2. Open WhatsApp on the iPhone.
  3. Go to Linked Devices, inside WhatsApp Settings on the iPhone.
  4. Choose Link a Device.
  5. Point the iPhone camera toward the QR code displayed on the Mac.

When the scan is complete, WhatsApp will open on the Mac with your conversations.

Preparing the Mac

Before touching the iPhone, prepare the Mac. Open WhatsApp and confirm that the QR code screen is visible. Do not close that window. If the screen locks or the code expires, WhatsApp may show a new code; this is normal. Make sure the Mac screen brightness is not too low. You may not need to see it, but the iPhone camera does need to capture the code clearly.

Preparing the iPhone

  1. Unlock the iPhone.
  2. Open WhatsApp.
  3. At the bottom of the screen, find the Settings tab.
  4. Go to Linked Devices.
  5. Find Link a Device.
  6. Double tap with VoiceOver to activate it. The iPhone may ask for Face ID, Touch ID, or passcode authentication.
  7. The camera will open to scan the QR code.

Scanning the QR code

When the camera is active, point the iPhone toward the Mac screen. There is no need to press a capture button. The iPhone detects the code automatically when it is properly framed.

As a practical guide, place the iPhone approximately 20 to 40 centimeters from the Mac screen, aimed toward the center. If it does not scan on the first try, move the iPhone slowly. The camera needs a moment to focus.

If you have some usable vision or someone nearby who can assist, it may be enough for that person to confirm that the iPhone is pointing at the code. They do not need to touch anything or access your account.

What happens when linking is complete

When the iPhone recognizes the QR code, the process finishes automatically. On the Mac, WhatsApp stops showing the linking screen and begins loading your chats. On the iPhone, the Mac appears as a linked device inside WhatsApp. From then on, you can use WhatsApp on the Mac without scanning the code every time. The iPhone remains the main device, while the Mac is authorized as an additional device.

If the QR code expires

Sometimes the QR code expires if you take too long. WhatsApp will show a new one or let you refresh it. This is not a failure; simply scan the new code.

If the iPhone cannot scan the code

  1. Confirm that WhatsApp is still open on the Mac and that the QR code is still visible.
  2. Increase the Mac screen brightness.
  3. Move the iPhone slightly closer or farther away and hold it steady for a few seconds.
  4. Clean the iPhone camera if needed.
  5. Check that there are no strong reflections on the screen.
  6. Close and reopen WhatsApp on the Mac if the code seems unresponsive.
  7. Verify in iPhone Settings that WhatsApp has permission to use the camera.

Checking that the Mac is linked

After linking, you can check from the iPhone: open WhatsApp, go to Settings, then Linked Devices. The Mac should appear in the list. From that same screen, you can log out of the Mac if needed later.

When to log out of the Mac

Log out if you stop using that Mac, share it with others, sell it, take it for repair, or suspect someone could access your conversations. On the iPhone, open WhatsApp, go to Settings, Linked Devices, select the Mac, and choose Log Out.

Common linking mistakes

  1. Thinking the QR code must be typed.
  2. Trying to scan the code with the regular iPhone Camera app instead of from WhatsApp.
  3. Not going to Linked Devices.
  4. Moving the iPhone too much while scanning.
  5. Closing WhatsApp on the Mac before finishing.
  6. Thinking the process failed because VoiceOver does not read the QR code as text.

Asking for help to aim the camera can be reasonable. Handing over full control of the phone without knowing what the other person is doing is something else entirely. The WhatsApp account is still yours.

Module 2 exercise

  1. Open WhatsApp on the Mac and leave the QR code visible.
  2. Open WhatsApp on the iPhone.
  3. Go to Settings, then Linked Devices.
  4. Choose Link a Device.
  5. Scan the QR code on the Mac.
  6. Wait for your chats to load on the Mac.
  7. Return to the iPhone and confirm that the Mac appears in the linked devices list.

By the end of this module, WhatsApp should be linked on the Mac and your conversations should appear in the application. If you see the chat list on the Mac, linking is complete.

Module 3. Basic VoiceOver commands needed for this course

Before going fully into WhatsApp, it is worth pausing briefly on VoiceOver. Not to turn this course into a complete Mac manual, but to make sure the minimum tools used throughout are clear. WhatsApp for Mac is not handled like an iPhone app. Here you do not explore the screen with your finger; you work mainly with the keyboard, focus, windows, groups, lists, buttons, text fields, and areas that sometimes require interaction.

The VO command

VoiceOver uses a key combination called the VO command. On most Macs, it is Control plus Option. If you have configured Caps Lock as the VO key, you can use it instead. When this course says “VO plus,” it always refers to this combination.

Essential commands

These are the commands you will use most often during the course:

Moving through elements

  1. VO plus Right Arrow: move to the next element.
  2. VO plus Left Arrow: move to the previous element.

Activating an element

  1. VO plus Spacebar: activate the button, control, or element currently selected by VoiceOver.

Interacting and stopping interaction

  1. VO plus Shift plus Down Arrow: interact with an area, group, list, or table to explore its internal content.
  2. VO plus Shift plus Up Arrow: stop interacting and return to the previous level.

Repeating and reading

  1. VO plus F3: hear the current element again.
  2. VO plus A: read from the current position forward.

Opening the context menu

  1. VO plus Shift plus M: open the context menu for the selected element. Many WhatsApp actions, such as reply, forward, copy, delete, mute, and archive, are found in this menu.

Item chooser

  1. VO plus I: open the item chooser, which shows buttons, controls, and other elements available in the window.
  2. Escape: close the chooser or cancel an action.

Keyboard help

  1. VO plus K: open VoiceOver keyboard help. It lets you press keys and hear what they do without executing the action.
  2. Escape: exit keyboard help.

Switching apps and windows

  1. Command plus Tab: switch between open applications.
  2. Command plus W: close the current window.
  3. Command plus Q: quit the application completely.

Searching

  1. Command plus F: open search inside the application, if available in that context.
  2. Command plus Space: open Spotlight to search for applications.

Text editing

  1. Command plus A: select all text in the active field.
  2. Command plus C: copy.
  3. Command plus X: cut.
  4. Command plus V: paste.
  5. Command plus Z: undo, if available.
  6. Enter: send the message when focus is in the WhatsApp message field.
  7. Shift plus Enter: insert a line break inside a message, if your WhatsApp version allows it.

Tab

  1. Tab: move forward through interactive controls.
  2. Shift plus Tab: move backward through controls.

Do not rely only on Tab. On the Mac, you will often need to combine it with VoiceOver commands. A good rule is this: use Tab to move quickly through controls; use VO plus arrow keys to explore in more detail; if VoiceOver announces a group or area with internal content, interact with it.

What to do when focus is lost

In WhatsApp, VoiceOver may sometimes seem to be somewhere unexpected. When that happens, do not press random keys:

  1. Listen to what VoiceOver announces.
  2. Use VO plus Left or Right Arrow to orient yourself.
  3. If you are inside an area, stop interacting with VO plus Shift plus Up Arrow.
  4. If you need to explore an area, interact with VO plus Shift plus Down Arrow.
  5. Try Tab or Shift plus Tab to find main controls.
  6. If nothing responds, switch to another app with Command plus Tab and return to WhatsApp.
  7. If the problem continues, close the window or restart WhatsApp.

Commands to practice before continuing

Before continuing with WhatsApp, practice these commands in any Mac window: VO plus Right Arrow, VO plus Left Arrow, VO plus Spacebar, VO plus Shift plus Down Arrow, VO plus Shift plus Up Arrow, Tab, Shift plus Tab, VO plus Shift plus M, VO plus I, Command plus Tab, Command plus W, and Command plus Q.

You do not need perfect mastery. But the commands should not feel unfamiliar. If every command requires half a minute of thought, using WhatsApp will become slower and heavier than necessary.

Module 3 exercise

  1. Open a simple application such as Finder.
  2. Move through the window with VO plus Right Arrow and VO plus Left Arrow.
  3. Find an area or group and interact with VO plus Shift plus Down Arrow.
  4. Leave that area with VO plus Shift plus Up Arrow.
  5. Find a button and activate it with VO plus Spacebar.
  6. Open the item chooser with VO plus I and close it with Escape.
  7. Switch to another application with Command plus Tab and return to the previous one.

By the end of this module, you should know the basic VoiceOver commands used in this course. You do not need to be a VoiceOver expert, but you should understand four core ideas: VoiceOver moves by elements, some areas require interaction, you must stop interacting to leave an area, and focus always matters.

Module 4. Understanding the WhatsApp for Mac window

Once WhatsApp is installed and linked, the next step is understanding the main window. This module is more important than it may seem. Many later problems do not begin when sending a file, recording audio, or searching for a message; they begin earlier, when the user does not know which area they are in.

WhatsApp for Mac does not have the touch simplicity of the iPhone. It is a desktop app with several distinct areas. Knowing them in advance prevents a good deal of confusion.

Main areas of the window

The WhatsApp for Mac window is generally organized into these areas:

  1. Left sidebar: contains the list of chats, groups, and conversations. It may also include navigation icons or buttons for different sections.
  2. Central conversation area: shows the messages in the open chat.
  3. Message field: the lower area where you type a message.
  4. Toolbar: may include buttons for attaching files, recording audio, inserting emoji, or other actions.
  5. Top area or chat header: may show the contact or group name, call buttons, or additional information.

The exact layout may vary between versions. Explore it with VoiceOver instead of assuming it will always look the same.

Exploring the window with VoiceOver

  1. Press VO plus Right Arrow or VO plus Left Arrow to move through elements.
  2. When VoiceOver announces a group, list, or table, interact with VO plus Shift plus Down Arrow to explore inside it.
  3. If you are somewhere you do not recognize, stop interacting with VO plus Shift plus Up Arrow and explore again.
  4. Use Tab to jump through main interactive controls.
  5. Use VO plus I to open the item chooser and get an overview of available elements.

Knowing where you are

  1. If you hear contact or group names, you are in the chat list.
  2. If you hear message content, you are in the conversation area.
  3. If VoiceOver announces a text field or edit field, you are in the message field.
  4. If you hear buttons such as Attach, Emoji, Audio, or Send, you are near the toolbar.

If you are unsure where you are, press VO plus F3 to hear the current element again.

Common orientation mistakes

  1. Thinking you are reading messages when you are still in the chat list.
  2. Typing before confirming focus is in the message field.
  3. Not interacting with areas that require it.
  4. Not stopping interaction before looking for another area.
  5. Assuming the structure will be identical across versions.

Module 4 exercise

  1. Open WhatsApp on the Mac.
  2. Move through the window with VO plus Right Arrow.
  3. Identify the chat list.
  4. Interact with the list if necessary.
  5. Open a chat and identify the conversation area.
  6. Find the message field.
  7. Close WhatsApp and repeat the exploration.

By the end of this module, you should have a mental map of the WhatsApp for Mac window: where the chat list is, where the conversation is, where the message field is, and how to move between these areas with VoiceOver.

Module 5. Moving between chats without getting lost

Moving between conversations is one of the most frequent actions in WhatsApp. If you do not master it, every task can feel like walking through unfamiliar rooms while VoiceOver announces things you did not expect. This module focuses on navigating the chat list, opening conversations, returning to the list, and switching chats safely.

Finding the chat list

The chat list may require interaction. In the main WhatsApp window, move with VO plus Right or Left Arrow until VoiceOver announces something related to chats, conversations, a list, or another name suggesting that area. If VoiceOver announces a group or table, interact with VO plus Shift plus Down Arrow to enter and move through conversations.

Opening a chat

Once in the chat list, move with the arrow keys until you find the chat you want. When VoiceOver announces it, activate it with VO plus Spacebar or Enter, depending on your configuration. Then listen carefully: if it begins reading messages, you are in the conversation. If it continues reading chat names, you are still in the list. If it announces another element, you need to reorient.

Returning to the chat list

There are several strategies:

  1. Stop interacting if you are inside the conversation, then move with VO arrows until you find the chat list.
  2. Use Tab and Shift plus Tab from the message field or nearby buttons to move back toward chats.
  3. Use VO plus I and look for an item related to chats, lists, or conversations.
  4. If focus is completely disoriented, close and reopen the window as a last resort.

Searching for a chat by name

When there are many conversations, search is often faster than browsing the list. Try Command plus F. If a search field opens, type the contact or group name and review results with VoiceOver or the arrow keys. You do not always need the full name; part of it may be enough.

The search field may be announced as Search, Search or start new chat, Search chats, Search field, or something similar.

Archived and pinned chats

Pinned chats appear at the top of the list, regardless of recent activity. Archived chats may not appear in the main list. If a chat does not appear, check for an Archived section, which may be near the top or bottom of the list. To return an archived chat to the main list, open its options and unarchive it.

Unread messages

When a chat has unread messages, VoiceOver may announce this with the chat name, the number of messages, the word unread, or an indicator. Pay attention to this so you do not miss pending conversations.

Orientation routine

  1. First, identify whether you are in the chat list.
  2. If not, explore the window until you find it.
  3. Open the desired chat.
  4. Confirm that the correct conversation is open.
  5. Find the message field only after you know where you are.

This routine may feel slow at first. Later it becomes automatic, and the classic beginner errors, such as typing in the wrong place or activating something blindly, almost disappear by themselves.

Common mistakes

  1. Opening a chat and typing without confirming the text field.
  2. Thinking you are in the chat list when you are actually in the conversation.
  3. Not interacting with the list when VoiceOver requires it.
  4. Not leaving one area before looking for another.
  5. Searching for a chat using a name different from the saved contact name.
  6. Forgetting that a chat may be archived.

Module 5 exercise

  1. Open WhatsApp on the Mac.
  2. Find the chat list.
  3. Move through at least five conversations.
  4. Open a chat and confirm you are inside the conversation.
  5. Return to the chat list.
  6. Open another chat.
  7. Return again to the chat list.
  8. Search for a chat using the search field.

Module 16. Managing chats

Using WhatsApp is not only reading and replying. It also means keeping some order. Otherwise the main list fills with old conversations, noisy groups, pending messages, chats that no longer matter, and contacts appearing exactly where they are least needed.

Archive and unarchive

Archiving hides a conversation from the main list without deleting it. The messages remain and you can recover the chat later. To archive, open the context menu on the chat and choose Archive. To recover it, find the Archived section, open the chat options, and choose Unarchive.

Pin and unpin

Pinning keeps an important chat at the top of the list. Open the context menu and choose Pin. To remove it from the top, choose Unpin. Do not pin too many chats. If everything is urgent, nothing is.

Muting stops or reduces notifications from a chat without leaving it or deleting messages. Open the context menu and choose Mute, then select a duration if offered. To reverse it, choose Unmute, Turn on notifications, or a similar option.

Mark as unread helps you remember to return to a conversation later. Mark as read removes the unread state. Both options may appear in the context menu.

Delete, clear, and leave

Deleting a chat removes the conversation from your WhatsApp view. Clearing a chat removes messages but may keep the chat available. Leaving a group removes you from the group. Good management habits

  1. Pin only truly important chats.
  2. Archive conversations that are finished.

  4. Mark as unread what requires a later response.
  5. Save important files outside WhatsApp.
  6. Delete only when you are sure.

Module 18. Notifications in macOS

Notifications are useful when they tell you what matters and a nuisance when they turn the Mac into a nervous radio. In WhatsApp for Mac, notifications depend on two places: WhatsApp settings and macOS notification settings. If either side is wrong, alerts may not arrive, may not sound, may show no content, or may be blocked by Focus mode.

Two levels of notifications

First, WhatsApp must have its own notifications enabled. Second, macOS must allow WhatsApp to send notifications. If WhatsApp allows them but macOS blocks them, they will not work properly. If macOS allows them but WhatsApp disables them, the result is just as disappointing.

Inside WhatsApp

Open WhatsApp settings and find Notifications. Review sounds, message notifications, group notifications, previews, calls, and any available tones. Availability may vary.

In macOS

Open System Settings, go to Notifications, find WhatsApp, and confirm notifications are allowed. Review alert style, whether they appear on the lock screen, whether they appear in Notification Center, whether they play sound, and whether previews are shown.

Focus mode

If WhatsApp suddenly stops alerting you, check whether a Focus mode such as Do Not Disturb, Work, or Sleep is active. Focus can silence notifications even when WhatsApp and macOS settings appear correct.

Groups and notification overload

Very active groups can produce too many alerts. Mute groups that do not need immediate attention. This keeps important notifications active without letting one group hijack the day. Attention is limited; WhatsApp does not always behave as if it knows that.

If notifications do not arrive

  1. Check the Mac Internet connection.
  2. Check WhatsApp notification settings.
  3. Check macOS notification settings for WhatsApp.
  4. Check Focus mode.
  5. Check whether the specific chat is muted.
  6. Check the volume and audio output.
  7. Close and reopen WhatsApp.
  8. Restart the Mac if needed.

If there are too many notifications

  1. Mute very active groups.
  2. Turn off group sounds if available.
  3. Use Focus mode at certain times.
  4. Archive chats you do not need to see.
  5. Keep alerts only for what matters.

Module 19. Privacy and security

WhatsApp contains personal conversations, documents, photos, audio, contacts, links, groups, and often more sensitive information than it seems at first glance. Using WhatsApp on the Mac is not just about knowing how to write or send files. It also means knowing what stays open, who can access it, what permissions are granted, and how to log out when needed.

Why privacy matters on WhatsApp for Mac

When you link WhatsApp to the Mac, that computer is authorized to access your conversations. Anyone who can use your Mac session may be able to open WhatsApp and see chats, files, groups, and messages. Security therefore depends not only on WhatsApp, but also on how the Mac user account is protected.

Reviewing linked devices

On the iPhone, open WhatsApp, go to Settings, then Linked Devices, and review the list. If you see a device you do not recognize, log out of it immediately. This review is simple and worth doing from time to time.

Logging out of the Mac

Closing the WhatsApp window is not logging out. Quitting the app is not necessarily unlinking it either. To log out clearly, use the iPhone: WhatsApp, Settings, Linked Devices, select the Mac, and choose Log Out. Do this before selling, lending, repairing, or sharing the Mac.

Protecting the Mac account

Use a password on your Mac user account. Do not leave the Mac unlocked. Enable automatic locking when you step away. If other people use the same Mac, each person should ideally have a separate user account.

Verification codes and QR codes

Never share WhatsApp verification codes. Not by phone, not by message, not with someone claiming to be support. Also, do not scan QR codes you do not understand. Scan only the QR code shown on your own Mac when linking your own account. A QR code is not modern decoration; it may be a doorway.

Two-step verification

Two-step verification adds a PIN to your WhatsApp account. It is usually configured from the iPhone. Use a PIN you can remember, add a recovery email if offered, and do not share the PIN. Avoid storing it in a note named WhatsApp PIN, which is the kind of generous gift problems enjoy receiving.

Privacy options

Review who can see your profile photo, last seen, online status, about text, status updates, read receipts, and who can add you to groups. If you communicate with many people you do not know personally, more restrictive options may be appropriate.

Links, files, and groups

Do not open suspicious links, even if they come from someone you know. Their account may be compromised, or they may have forwarded something without checking. Do not open unexpected files from unknown senders. In groups, remember that all participants can see what you send. Before sharing files, images, or private information, confirm the group is correct.

Getting help without exposing chats

If someone helps you, keep control of the iPhone when possible. Ask for specific help, such as aiming the camera or locating a button. Do not show verification codes, private chats, or sensitive files unnecessarily.

If you suspect access to your account

  1. Open WhatsApp on the iPhone.
  2. Go to Settings and Linked Devices.
  3. Log out of any device you do not recognize.
  4. Log out of all devices if in doubt.
  5. Review two-step verification.
  6. Review iPhone and Mac security.
  7. Change passwords if you suspect device access.

Module 21. Common problems and solutions

WhatsApp for Mac can work well for days and then suddenly behave as if someone moved all the furniture while you were out. Sometimes the problem is WhatsApp. Sometimes macOS. Sometimes VoiceOver. Sometimes a missing permission. And sometimes, because apparently simplicity was not invited, it is a mixture of all of them.

General rule

Check the simple things first: focus, interaction, permissions, connection, and whether the app needs restarting. Only then move toward bigger measures such as unlinking, reinstalling, or changing settings more deeply.

WhatsApp does not open

Check that WhatsApp is installed in Applications, open it with Spotlight, restart the Mac, check for updates, and reinstall from an official source if needed. Do not delete iPhone conversations because of a Mac opening problem.

WhatsApp asks to link again

If the QR code appears again, the Mac is no longer linked or the session has closed. Link it again from the iPhone: WhatsApp, Settings, Linked Devices, Link a Device, and scan the QR code.

The QR code does not appear or cannot be scanned

If the code does not appear, wait, explore the window, confirm WhatsApp is active, close and reopen it, and check the Internet connection. If the iPhone cannot scan it, increase Mac brightness, clean the iPhone camera, adjust distance, hold still, check camera permission, and refresh the code if it expires.

Chats or messages do not read correctly

Confirm the chat is open, find the conversation area, interact if needed, leave other areas if trapped, use Tab, the item chooser, or switch apps and return. Do not reinstall immediately for a focus problem. First orient; then escalate.

The message field cannot be found

Check that a conversation is open. Move near the lower part of the chat with VO arrows or Tab. Listen for text field, edit field, or type a message. If you are interacting with messages, stop interacting and search again.

Typing or sending fails

If typing does nothing, focus is not in the field. If Enter does not send, find the Send button, check that the field contains text, and verify Internet connection. If you sent too soon, use Shift plus Enter next time or draft in Notes.

Reply, forward, or context menu options are missing

Make sure focus is on the correct element. The context menu on a chat is different from the context menu on a message or file. Move one element before or after and try VO plus Shift plus M again.

Attach or file sending problems

Find the Attach button near the message field or use the item chooser. If a selected file does not send, look for the final Send button. If you cannot find a file, locate it first in Finder and place it in an easy folder. If it is in iCloud, make sure it is downloaded locally.

Files cannot be opened or found

If a received file will not open, check whether it fully downloaded and whether you have a compatible app. Try opening it from Finder. If you cannot find a downloaded file, check Downloads, Recents, search by name, sort by modification date, or use Show in Finder if available.

Voice messages do not work

Check microphone permission, active input device, and whether VoiceOver is being recorded through speakers. Use headphones when recording if VoiceOver audio leaks into the message. If received audio does not play, check the Play button, volume, audio output, and whether AirPods are connected to the Mac or iPhone.

Calls do not work

Check microphone and camera permissions, connection, audio devices, and whether the buttons are available in the top area of the chat. For urgent calls, use the iPhone if the Mac interface is difficult.

Notifications do not arrive or arrive too often

Check WhatsApp notification settings, macOS notification settings, Focus mode, muted chats, volume, and audio output. If too many arrive, mute active groups, disable group sounds if available, use Focus, and archive low-priority chats.

Search and group reading problems

For missing chats, search by name, surname, or number and check Archived. For old messages, search within the chat using concrete words. In groups, slow down VoiceOver and read message by message, identifying the sender before replying.

Privacy concerns

If you suspect account access, open Linked Devices on the iPhone, log out of unknown devices, review two-step verification, and check iPhone and Mac security. If getting help, do not expose private chats, files, or codes unnecessarily.

General troubleshooting routine

  1. Listen to what VoiceOver announces.
  2. Check where focus is.
  3. Interact or stop interacting as needed.
  4. Check permissions.
  5. Check Internet connection.
  6. Close and reopen WhatsApp.
  7. Restart the Mac.
  8. Update WhatsApp.
  9. Unlink and relink only if the session is affected.
  10. Reinstall only when earlier steps fail.
