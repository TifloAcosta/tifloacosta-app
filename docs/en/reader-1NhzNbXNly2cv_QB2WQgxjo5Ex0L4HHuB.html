Apple folds the iPhone, splits the calendar and distributes intelligence by country: everything new from the 2026 keynote

An article by Tony Acosta for TifloAcosta.

Apple managed something rather curious this year: it introduced an iPhone that folds while also forcing us to unfold a map to work out which features we will actually get depending on the device, language and place where we live.

I am Tony Acosta and, as we usually do at TifloAcosta, we are not going to judge a presentation by the number of cores inside a processor or by how many impressive names Apple can invent for a camera. That is fine for technical specification sheets. I am much more interested in what changes when we take the device out of the box and start using it.

The keynote on September 9, 2026 left us with five main protagonists: iPhone 18 Pro, iPhone 18 Pro Max, Apple's first foldable iPhone, called iPhone Duo, Apple Watch Series 12, Apple Watch Ultra 4 and AirPods 5. Apple also confirmed that iOS 27 and watchOS 27 will arrive on September 14. And no, I have not forgotten the regular iPhone 18. Apple is the one that forgot to introduce it... for now.

Prices in this article are shown in US dollars only as a common reference. The final price will depend on each market, local taxes, currency and those little surprises that appear whenever the same device crosses a border.

iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max: improving what already works without pretending the phone has been invented again

The iPhone 18 Pro starts at 1,199 dollars and the Pro Max at 1,299 dollars, both with 256 GB of storage. For the first time, configurations can go up to 2 TB. Pre-orders begin on September 12 and general availability starts on September 18.

Apple introduces the A20 Pro processor. It is built using 2-nanometer technology, and from this point we could happily disappear into CPU, GPU, Neural Engine and the rest of the family. I will spare you the excursion.

What matters is that Apple has improved power and efficiency, especially for artificial intelligence tasks running on the device, graphics, photo processing and sustained workloads. Internal cooling has also been improved so that all that power does not turn the iPhone into a small appliance designed mainly to warm your hand.

Dynamic Island is smaller and can display up to three Live Activities at the same time. It will not change anyone's life, but it is one of those refinements that shows how Apple keeps adjusting the interface.

The most interesting camera change, mainly because it can be explained without wearing a lab coat, is the variable aperture on the main 48-megapixel camera. A camera needs to control how much light enters. Until now, phones handled much of this with computational processing. On the new Pro models, there is also a physical mechanism that can open or close the amount of light coming in, rather like the pupil of an eye.

What does that actually do? It should improve performance in different lighting conditions and offer better control over which parts of a scene remain in focus. That is more useful to most people than memorising the name of the sensor.

Battery life is also improved, especially on the Pro Max, and Apple has introduced faster charging. The new C2 modem also promises better 5G performance and improved efficiency.

A photo that can prove it has not been through the artificial intelligence beauty salon

The iPhone 18 Pro models include Apple Reference Image, a particularly interesting feature at a time when we can look at a photograph and no longer be quite sure whether we are seeing reality, artificial creation or our brother-in-law magically made thirty years younger.

The system can create a cryptographic reference for the original photograph. If the image is later altered with artificial intelligence, that digital negative can be preserved so the edited image can be compared with the original and its origin can be verified.

And here comes the first border of the day: Apple Reference Image will not initially be available in the European Union or China.

This will be a recurring theme throughout the article: the fact that a feature belongs to the phone does not automatically mean it belongs to our phone.

iPhone Duo: Apple discovers foldables... seven years later

And now we reach the star of the show.

Apple has officially introduced the iPhone Duo, its first foldable phone. Samsung and other manufacturers have been in this market for years, so we can safely rule out Apple arriving first without opening a criminal investigation. Still, Apple often enters categories late because it wants to integrate them in its own way, and this is where we will have to see whether the wait was worth it.

The iPhone Duo closes into something roughly the size and shape of a passport. On the outside it has a 5.4-inch display, and when opened it reveals a 7.6-inch inner display, the largest ever used on an iPhone. It starts at 1,999 dollars with 256 GB and can be configured up to 2 TB. Pre-orders begin on October 16 and sales begin on October 23.

This is where the way we use an iPhone genuinely changes.

iOS 27 adapts apps when the device is opened, allows two apps to run side by side and rearranges controls and content depending on how the phone is being held. It can also work partially folded, show content on the outer display in certain positions and use the larger screen in a way that starts to bring the iPhone closer to iPad territory.

Apple has also announced Apple Pencil support, although that support will arrive later.

And there is another surprise: Face ID is not the main biometric system here. The iPhone Duo brings back Touch ID in the side button and allows several fingers to be registered. It can also be unlocked with an Apple Watch.

Personally, I do not automatically see Touch ID as a step backwards. For a blind person, having a physical, easy-to-locate point for authentication can be genuinely convenient. But this needs to be tested, which is a sentence those of us who write about technology should probably use much more often.

The iPhone Duo has not yet taken its VoiceOver exam

Here I want to make a clear distinction between what Apple has announced and what still needs to be checked.

It is all very well that iOS visually adapts an app when moving from a small display to a large one. For VoiceOver users, the questions are different: will focus remain stable when the device is opened or closed? How will VoiceOver navigate two apps side by side? What happens when the interface moves because the phone is partially folded? Will navigation order remain logical even when visual elements reorganise themselves?

Apple has not yet demonstrated those situations in enough detail from a VoiceOver perspective. So I am not going to tell you that the iPhone Duo is wonderful for a blind person simply because that sentence would look good in a headline.

First we test it. Then we talk.

What we do know is that this is probably the biggest physical change to the iPhone in many years, which makes it especially important to see how Apple has handled accessibility in an interface that no longer has a single fixed shape.

And what happened to the regular iPhone 18?

No. You did not miss a slide.

Apple did not introduce the standard iPhone 18.

For the first time, Apple is making a major split in the traditional iPhone release calendar. September brings the Pro models and the new Duo, while the standard iPhone 18, a new Air model and the iPhone 18e are expected during the first quarter of 2027.

In other words, Apple has not killed the iPhone 18. It has split the calendar.

And by pure coincidence it has kept the cheap part for later... sorry, I meant the Pro part for September.

AirPods 5: noise cancellation moves down a level

AirPods 5 arrive in two versions, and here the improvement is pleasantly easy to explain.

Active Noise Cancellation is no longer reserved for the more expensive model. Both AirPods 5 versions include Active Noise Cancellation, Adaptive Audio and Conversation Awareness. Apple also says they can remove up to 50 percent more outside noise than AirPods 4 with Active Noise Cancellation.

The base model costs 129 dollars. For 149 dollars you also get a wireless charging case and, particularly interesting, volume control by sliding a finger along the stem of the earbud. This is the first time Apple has brought that kind of control to its open AirPods design.

Pre-orders opened on the day of the presentation and the earbuds reach stores on September 18.

For those of us who depend heavily on audio, bringing noise cancellation to the entry model is probably more relevant than many of the decorative flourishes that can appear on a keynote slide. And being able to change volume directly on the earbud without chasing controls on a screen does not sound like a bad idea either.

Apple Watch Series 12: now it wants to know not only how much you move, but how you are doing

Apple Watch Series 12 keeps a starting price of 399 dollars, can be pre-ordered now and arrives on September 18. It introduces the S11 chip and a new generation of health-related sensors.

Apple increases how often the watch collects certain heart-related data and adds a new feature called Readiness.

The idea is easy enough to understand: instead of only telling us how much we slept, how much we trained or what our measurements are, the watch tries to combine that information and tell us whether our body seems ready for an intense workout, whether we should take it easier or whether recovery might be a better idea.

In other words, the watch is beginning to acquire a skill that some human beings lose rather easily: saying “maybe today is not the day to be a hero”.

Apple is also expanding cycle tracking, perimenopause and menopause-related features.

Audio Intelligence: the watch starts listening in order to help

Apple Watch Series 12 and Ultra 4 also introduce a set of features called Audio Intelligence.

Sound Recognition can identify certain environmental sounds, such as an alarm, doorbell or crying baby. That has obvious value for Deaf and hard-of-hearing users.

Live Rewind lets the user double-press the Digital Crown and read a transcription of roughly the previous 15 seconds. It can be useful when we did not understand something, missed a sentence or our brain simply decided to take those fifteen seconds off without filing a request.

Siri Recap goes further by creating titles, summaries and key points from meetings or conversations so they can be reviewed later. Apple says the system does not create or store audio recordings.

And here comes another large sticker saying “please read the small print”: Live Rewind and Siri Recap will enter beta later this year and will begin in English. Apple says more languages will follow.

So once again, buying the watch on September 18 does not mean every one of these features will immediately start chatting to us in every language.

Apple Watch Ultra 4: more battery life and the same basic philosophy

Apple Watch Ultra 4 shares the new health and Audio Intelligence features of Series 12 but keeps its focus on sport, adventure and demanding use.

It starts at 799 dollars and will be available on September 18 in more than 50 countries.

Battery life increases to around 50 hours of normal use and 84 hours in Low Power Mode. It can also extend tracking time considerably for long-duration activities.

For VoiceOver users, battery life always matters. A screen reader is something we use constantly, not a feature we switch on twice a day to check a notification. That is why real battery improvements interest me rather more than a new titanium shade whose visual qualities, for reasons you can probably imagine, I am generously leaving to other people.

iOS 27 arrives on September 14

Apple has confirmed that iOS 27 will be available as a free update on Monday, September 14.

But there is an important distinction to make.

Being able to install iOS 27 is one thing.

Being able to use every Apple Intelligence feature is another.

And a third thing, because Apple apparently decided two layers of confusion were not entertaining enough, is being able to use every Siri AI feature.

iOS 27, Apple Intelligence and Siri AI are not the same thing

Apple Intelligence requires more recent hardware. Compatibility starts with the iPhone 15 Pro family and continues with later supported generations, along with other compatible Apple devices.

Apple Intelligence supports several languages, including English, Spanish, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Japanese, Korean, Chinese and others. But support for Apple Intelligence in a language does not mean every individual AI feature works in that language immediately. Some features have their own language restrictions.

Siri AI begins first in English. Other languages, including Spanish, are expected in a later expansion. Some features may also vary according to region, language and local regulation.

So “supported in Spanish” can mean “yes”, “not yet”, “this part yes”, “that part no” or “please come back next month”. Accuracy matters.

Europe and China get their own chapter again

Siri AI will not initially be available on iPhone, iPad or Apple Watch in the European Union.

China will also have restrictions affecting Siri AI and some Apple Intelligence features while Apple works through local regulatory requirements.

This is exactly what we need to avoid when talking about global technology: writing “Siri AI arrives with iOS 27” and then leaving millions of people searching their phones to find where on earth it has gone.

It arrives, yes.

But not in the same way for everyone.

Accessibility: this is one part of the presentation that interests me particularly

The accessibility features Apple has announced for this generation deserve more than a footnote.

With Apple Intelligence, VoiceOver gains a new image exploration experience capable of providing much more detailed descriptions of photographs, scanned documents, bills and other visual content.

Real-Time Recognition also takes an important step forward: a blind person will be able to use the iPhone Action button to ask what is in front of the camera and then ask follow-up questions using natural language.

Not simply “describe the image”. We will be able to continue asking: “Where is the door?”, “What does the label say?” or “Which of these two bottles looks like oil?”.

That changes the idea of visual recognition quite a lot because we move from receiving one closed description to having a small conversation about what the camera is seeing.

Magnifier gains equivalent capabilities for low-vision users, including voice requests such as increasing zoom or switching on the flashlight.

Voice Control learns to understand what we mean

Voice Control is also gaining natural-language interaction.

Until now, many interfaces required us to know the exact name of a control or use numbered overlays. With the new version, we can describe what we want to activate in a more natural way.

This also has an interesting indirect accessibility benefit: it may help when an app contains badly labelled controls.

Now for the part small headlines often forget: this Apple Intelligence-powered layer of Voice Control will initially be limited to selected English-speaking markets.

So no, Voice Control itself is not disappearing in other languages. It is this new intelligent layer that launches with limited availability.

The distinction matters.

Generated captions: excellent idea, much less global launch

Apple will also be able to generate captions automatically when a video does not already include them.

Processing takes place on the device and can be used with personal videos, content received from other people or videos played online.

The idea is excellent.

The launch is less so: initially it will only be available in selected English-speaking markets.

Apple is also introducing Name Recognition, which can alert a Deaf or hard-of-hearing person when someone says their name, and here the picture is very different: it will work across dozens of languages worldwide.

This contrast perfectly explains why TifloAcosta keeps insisting on languages. Two features can appear in the same accessibility section and still have completely different availability.

Accessibility Reader grows as well

Accessibility Reader will be able to handle more complex documents containing columns, images and tables, create summaries for a quick overview and translate content while preserving the user's visual and formatting preferences.

This is one of those improvements that may sound less spectacular during a keynote and turn out to be far more useful in everyday life.

Not everything is about making a phone perform artificial-intelligence fireworks. Sometimes the genuinely useful improvement is opening a complicated document and getting it to stop behaving as if it had been designed by someone who personally hated screen readers.

watchOS 27 also arrives on September 14

watchOS 27 will be available on September 14 for supported Apple Watch models.

It includes interface changes, new intelligent features and improvements that, in some cases, also depend on having an iPhone compatible with Apple Intelligence. Because if you thought we were finished with conditions, you clearly do not know Apple well enough yet.

What did not appear

The keynote ended without a new Apple TV, HomePod mini, home hub with a display or new Macs.

There are rumours about those products and some may arrive in another round of announcements over the coming months, but they were not introduced today. We will keep a healthy rule here: when Apple announces something, we talk about it. Until then, a rumour remains a rumour.

So, was this an important keynote?

Yes.

But not because Apple managed to build another faster processor. That happens almost every year and at some point we may have to stop pretending to be surprised.

The importance of this presentation lies elsewhere.

For the first time, the physical shape of the iPhone changes dramatically with the Duo. Apple is also splitting the iPhone release calendar and leaving the standard model for another time. AirPods bring Active Noise Cancellation to a lower price. Apple Watch is beginning to connect health, context and audio in a more ambitious way. And accessibility tools are increasingly being combined with intelligent models capable of describing, interpreting and answering questions.

But we have definitely entered an era where it is no longer enough to ask, “Is my iPhone compatible?”

  • Is my model compatible?
  • Is my language supported?
  • What about my language variant?
  • Is the feature available where I live?
  • Does it arrive on launch day or three months later?
  • Does it require Apple Intelligence?
  • Does it require Siri AI?
  • And is Mercury in retrograde?

That last one is not yet listed in Apple's technical documentation, but considering the direction of travel, I would not rule it out forever.

My conclusion

The September 2026 keynote strikes me as one of Apple's most interesting in several years because, finally, it is not entirely about making the same device slightly faster.

The iPhone Duo opens a completely new chapter and, for those of us who rely on accessibility, raises a lot of questions that I want to answer by actually testing the device rather than staring at photographs.

The new VoiceOver and Real-Time Recognition features could matter far more to a blind person than any conventional camera improvement. Being able to ask the iPhone about what is in front of us and continue asking questions about the scene could become an enormously useful tool.

But I also want to make one thing very clear: accessibility cannot be judged simply by reading Apple's list of features. We have to check languages, regions, models, real VoiceOver behaviour and, above all, what happens when the device reaches the hands of people who depend on those tools every day.

Apple has finished its presentation.

Now comes the interesting part: finding out how much of what was presented works exactly as described.

And if it does not work, do not worry. We will tell you that too.
