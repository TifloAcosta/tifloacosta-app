TifloAcosta now has an app: everything changes from here

Accessible written version of Tony Acosta’s video for Canal TifloAcosta.

This document follows the demonstration as recorded in the video, where TifloAcosta App is shown as version 1.2. Spanish interface labels heard in the recording are preserved and translated where useful.

Watch the original video on YouTube

Open TifloAcosta App

Introduction

In this video, Tony Acosta introduces TifloAcosta App, a web application created to bring TifloAcosta resources, documents, videos and other content together in one place.

The app is intended for use across different devices and operating systems, including iPhone, iPad, Android, Windows and Mac. The practical demonstration in the video is carried out on an iPhone with VoiceOver.

The recording itself is in Spanish. Whenever an interface label is important for following the demonstration, this written version keeps the Spanish wording heard in the video and gives its English meaning alongside it.

Installing TifloAcosta App on an iPhone

Tony starts from a saved link to TifloAcosta App. He explains that there is no need to memorize the address because the link will be made available to users.

He opens the link in Safari and then opens the Share menu, «Compartir» (Share).

In the share sheet, he finds and activates «Añadir a pantalla de inicio» (Add to Home Screen).

iOS then shows the confirmation screen. Tony checks the switch labelled «Abrir como app web» (Open as Web App) and makes sure it is turned on.

This allows TifloAcosta to open from its own Home Screen icon and behave as a standalone web app rather than simply as an ordinary Safari page.

He then finds and activates «Añadir» (Add). iOS returns to the Home Screen, where a new icon named «TifloAcosta» appears. Tony finds it with VoiceOver and opens it with a double tap.

The app’s first screen

When TifloAcosta App opens, VoiceOver first reaches «Saltar al contenido principal» (Skip to main content), a link that lets a screen-reader user move directly to the main part of the page.

At the top are the language controls. «Español» is selected, and the «English» button is beside it. Activating either option changes the language of the app interface.

The main heading is «TifloAcosta App». Beneath it, the Spanish interface displays the description «Recursos de accesibilidad y tecnología, organizados para llegar a ellos sin perderse por el camino», meaning that accessibility and technology resources are organized so users can reach what they need without getting lost along the way.

Searching for a resource

One of the first features Tony demonstrates is the search function. The app shows the section «Buscar recursos» (Search resources) and a field labelled «Título o palabra clave» (Title or keyword). The field suggests examples such as VoiceOver, Android and WhatsApp.

Tony enters the field, types «Android» and activates «Buscar» (Search). VoiceOver reports that five resources have been found.

Among the results are «Curso completo de Android con TalkBack» (Complete Android course with TalkBack) and «Curso Jieshuo en Android» (Jieshuo course on Android). The Spanish titles are preserved here because these are the names heard in the recording.

What you can do with a document

Tony opens the options for one of the resources. The app displays a dialog titled «Opciones del documento» (Document options).

The available actions are «Abrir documento» (Open document), «Descargar documento» (Download document), «Compartir documento» (Share document), «Añadir a favoritos» (Add to favorites) —or «Quitar de favoritos» (Remove from favorites) if it is already saved— and «Cancelar» (Cancel).

Open document displays the content. Download document starts the file download. Share document opens the sharing options provided by the device. Favorites make selected resources easier to find again later.

If Tony does not want to perform an action, he uses «Cancelar» (Cancel) to close the dialog. The search area also includes «Limpiar resultados» (Clear results), which removes the current search results.

Browsing by category and using favorites

Users do not need to know a document title in advance. TifloAcosta App also includes «Explorar recursos» (Explore resources).

The category selector initially reads «Seleccionar una categoría» (Select a category). Opening it reveals the available subject areas. Categories shown during the demonstration include Android, Apps and Apple Watch.

Next to the category selector is «Ver favoritos» (View favorites), which displays resources the user has previously marked as favorites.

What’s new

The Home screen also includes «Novedades» (What’s new), a section intended to highlight recently added or updated resources so they can be found quickly.

The YouTube channel inside the app

Another main section is headed «Canal TifloAcosta en YouTube» (TifloAcosta YouTube Channel). The app explains that users can access the full video catalog through a screen designed for comfortable searching, sorting and browsing.

The link «Explorar todos los vídeos» (Explore all videos) opens the integrated catalog. There is also a separate link for opening the TifloAcosta channel directly on YouTube.

This gives users an organized alternative way of reaching the channel’s videos from within the accessible structure of TifloAcosta App.

Tony’s book

The app also contains the section «Mi libro» (My book). The book shown is «La vida vista desde donde estoy», with the subtitle «Reflexiones desde una forma propia de estar en el mundo».

Because the book itself is in Spanish, its title and subtitle are kept exactly as they appear in the app. The section includes a short description and links for the print and Kindle editions on Amazon.

Contact and social links

TifloAcosta App also brings together different ways to contact Tony and reach the project’s other channels.

Options include WhatsApp and email, as well as links to TifloAcosta social networks and podcast services.

The aim is to keep these access points together instead of requiring users to find each one separately.

App settings

Tony later reaches «Configuración» (Settings). Inside it, he opens «Opciones de configuración visual» (Visual settings options), which contains controls for adapting the presentation of the content.

Text size

The first group changes text size. The available choices are «Predeterminado» (Default), «Grande» (Large), «Muy grande» (Extra large) and «Máximo» (Maximum). The app can follow the system settings while also saving TifloAcosta-specific preferences.

Color and contrast

The «Color y contraste» (Color and contrast) group contains «Perfil de contraste» (Contrast profile). The available options include «Seguir el sistema» (Follow system), «Alto contraste claro» (High-contrast light) and «Alto contraste oscuro» (High-contrast dark).

Line spacing

The «Separación entre líneas» (Line spacing) control offers «Normal» (Normal), «Cómoda» (Comfortable) and «Amplia» (Wide), allowing users to increase spacing when that makes text easier to read.

Stronger text

Under «Legibilidad» (Readability), the checkbox «Usar texto reforzado» enables stronger, heavier text for users who find it easier to see.

Resetting the settings

«Restablecer ajustes» (Reset settings) returns the visual preferences to their original values. Preferences that the user chooses can be retained for future sessions.

Notifications

TifloAcosta App also includes a «Notificaciones» (Notifications) section. The text in the app explains that notifications are intended for new resources, videos or other important updates.

Notifications are not enabled without the user’s choice. The app provides a control to activate them, and the browser requests permission when necessary.

Updating the app

The next section is «Actualización» (Update). At the time of the recording, the app displays «Versión actual: 1.2» (Current version: 1.2) and the button «Actualizar aplicación» (Update application).

Tony explains the updating system and presents this button as a way to check for and apply an updated version when needed.

A web app designed to keep growing

Near the end of the video, Tony explains that TifloAcosta App is a PWA, or Progressive Web App. This approach allows it to work across several platforms without initially depending on an app store.

He says the project will continue to be improved and made more professional, with the possibility of eventually bringing it to stores such as the App Store and Google Play.

Tony invites users to send feedback, suggestions and reports of any problems they encounter, and he reminds viewers of the different ways to follow and take part in TifloAcosta.

Conclusion

The video presents TifloAcosta App as a common entry point for the different parts of the TifloAcosta project.

From an iPhone with VoiceOver, the demonstration shows how to add the app to the Home Screen, open it, search for a document, use its document options, browse by category or favorites, open the YouTube catalog, reach information about the book and contact channels, and adjust several display preferences.

It also covers notifications and the app update section. This written adaptation is intended to let someone who cannot access the video’s pictures, sound, or either of them understand what Tony explains, what he does on screen and what the main controls are for.
