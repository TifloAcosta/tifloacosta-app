iOS 26.3: What's New?

Accessible written version of a Canal TifloAcosta video. Watch the original video on YouTube.

A tour of iOS 26.3

Tony uses this video to go through the changes he finds after installing iOS 26.3. Before opening Settings, he reminds viewers of the different ways the Canal TifloAcosta community stays in touch and receives updates.

He also makes an important point at the outset: not every feature is available on every iPhone or in every region. Some depend on the exact model, some on carrier support, and others on regulatory requirements in particular territories.

For accessibility, Tony says he has not found new user-facing accessibility features in this release and notes that the major accessibility additions arrived with the original iOS 26 release.

TifloAcosta channels and community

Tony mentions the Tiflo Acosta Facebook page, as well as the channel's presence on Instagram and X.

He explains that WhatsApp is the main channel for following developments closely and taking part in the community. To request access, he gives the number +34 603 516 398 and asks people to include their first and last name, where they are writing from, and the word alta.

Bluetooth and third-party accessories

In Settings > Bluetooth, Tony discusses changes being prepared to make some third-party headphones, hearing devices, and other accessories integrate more naturally with iPhone, particularly in the European Union.

The broader idea is that iPhone is gradually reducing some of the differences between Apple's own accessories and compatible products from other manufacturers.

This section needs an important timing clarification, included in the update note: proximity-triggered pairing for supported third-party accessories did not become available to EU users until a later iOS release.

Limiting precise location information available to mobile networks

Tony opens the mobile data settings and explains a privacy option designed to reduce the precision of location information that the cellular network itself can derive.

He emphasises that this is separate from the location an app receives through Location Services. The purpose is to limit certain information that a mobile network can use to estimate where the device is located.

In the video, he notes that availability depends both on the iPhone model and on carrier support.

Transferring from iPhone to Android

One of the clearest additions shown in the video appears under Settings > General > Transfer or Reset iPhone.

Alongside the familiar options, Tony finds Transfer to Android and activates it.

A screen opens with the heading Place Devices Next to Each Other, along with a Cancel button.

The system explains that the iPhone and Android device should be placed near one another so they can connect and begin the transfer process.

What can be transferred

Tony reviews the information that the system says can be moved to the new device.

The items mentioned include photos, messages, apps, the phone number, and other compatible data.

The screen also warns that some information is excluded, including certain Health data, Bluetooth-paired devices, and protected items.

Transfer requirements

Tony explains the requirements shown by the system: both devices need compatible, up-to-date software, a Wi-Fi connection, and Bluetooth enabled.

After reviewing the screen, he activates Cancel and exits without completing a transfer.

The video therefore demonstrates that the feature exists and shows its initial workflow, but it does not perform a complete real-world migration.

Greater openness toward third-party watches and accessories

Tony also discusses Apple's gradual move toward allowing watches and accessories from other manufacturers to make better use of iPhone features.

The aim is to improve interoperability with devices that do not belong to Apple's own ecosystem.

In the video, this is presented as an emerging direction. The update note below clarifies when notification-forwarding and proximity-pairing capabilities for third-party accessories later became available to EU users.

Privacy & Security: Location Services

Tony opens Settings > Privacy & Security > Location Services, where iOS lists apps and system features that can use location information.

Among the entries, he highlights one related to the Weather wallpaper.

Tony explains that this permission is connected with allowing the weather-based wallpaper to use location when needed to display the relevant conditions.

Not every update is visible as a headline feature

Tony reminds viewers that an update such as iOS 26.3 is not limited to visible changes in Settings. It also includes bug fixes and security changes that may not be noticeable during everyday use.

His walkthrough therefore focuses on the changes a user can actually find and identify on the device.

Update note — September 8, 2026

Release date and scope. Apple released iOS 26.3 on February 11, 2026. In its public update notes, Apple described it primarily as an update containing important bug fixes and security updates. Apple documented several of the related capabilities separately.

Transfer to Android. Apple confirms that iOS 26.3 introduced the ability to transfer data and an eSIM from an iPhone or iPad to an Android device. Supported data can include photos, contacts, calendars, call history, and messages. The feature requires a compatible Android device and Android version; Apple currently specifies compatible Android 17. Third-party app data can transfer only when the app developer enables support.

Limit Precise Location for cellular networks. The feature remains available, but current compatibility is more specific than the early description in the video. Apple currently lists iPhone Air, iPhone 16e, and iPhone 17e, along with certain cellular iPad models. With a SIM or eSIM from a carrier in the European Union or United Kingdom, the option can be enabled. The setting only reduces location precision available to cellular networks and does not change app permissions under Location Services.

Third-party proximity pairing. The ability for supported third-party Bluetooth accessories in the European Union to use a proximity-triggered discovery and pairing process similar to Apple's streamlined accessory setup was not available to customers in iOS 26.3. Apple documents customer availability beginning with iOS 26.5, and accessory manufacturers must implement and certify the capability.

Notifications and third-party watches. Apple developed new frameworks during this period that allow iPhone notifications to be forwarded to third-party accessories in the European Union. In iOS 26.4 these capabilities were still documented as developer-testing features that would reach customers in a future iOS 26 release. The video's comments are therefore best understood as the beginning of a broader interoperability effort, not as full compatibility with any third-party watch already available in iOS 26.3.

Accessibility. iOS 26.3 did not introduce a new collection of user-facing accessibility features comparable to the original iOS 26 release. Apple did, however, fix security and privacy vulnerabilities classified under the Accessibility component. These were security fixes rather than new VoiceOver or assistive-technology features.

The Siri prediction did not materialise in iOS 26.4. In the video, Tony refers to the expectation at the time that iOS 26.4 might bring deeper Gemini integration with Siri. iOS 26.4 was released on March 24, 2026 without that new Siri experience. Apple officially introduced Siri AI on June 8, 2026 as part of the operating-system 27 generation. Apple also announced that iPhone and iPad availability in the European Union would be delayed because of issues related to the Digital Markets Act.

Conclusion

Tony concludes that iOS 26.3 is an update with a relatively small number of visible additions, many of which depend on device model, region, carrier, or compatibility with other hardware.

The standout change demonstrated in the video is the new ability to begin a transfer from iPhone to Android directly from Settings, alongside the first steps toward broader interoperability with networks and third-party accessories.
