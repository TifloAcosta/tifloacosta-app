Two in one: checking suspicious messages and an app that tells you the history around you

Accessible written version of a Canal TifloAcosta video. Watch the original video on YouTube.

Two completely different tools

Tony brings together two ideas that have very little in common but can be useful in very different situations.

The first is a way to check a suspicious text, email, link, or phone number using Malwarebytes inside ChatGPT. The second is WikiTrip, an iPhone app that uses your location to tell you historical and cultural information about nearby places while you move around.

Part one: checking suspicious messages with Malwarebytes in ChatGPT

Tony starts with an everyday problem: messages, emails, or calls arrive from unfamiliar numbers, and it is not always easy to know whether they are legitimate, unwanted advertising, or an attempted scam.

To investigate, he opens ChatGPT on an iPhone with VoiceOver and demonstrates the Malwarebytes integration.

Adding Malwarebytes to ChatGPT

From ChatGPT's main screen, Tony opens the Menu and chooses Apps. He then uses the Search apps field and types malwarebytes.

Malwarebytes appears in the results. Tony explains that the service needs to be added or connected to ChatGPT. His own account is already connected, so he does not need to repeat the full setup.

He then closes the Apps screen and returns to a ChatGPT conversation for a real test.

Copying a suspicious text message

Tony switches to the Messages app and finds a suspicious SMS that he has received. The message says:

hola papa mi teléfono se me ha roto este va a ser mi número envíame un mensaje por WhatsApp en cuanto puedas o llámame

Using VoiceOver actions on the message, he finds Copy and activates it. The system confirms that the message has been copied.

A personal shortcut for typing @Malwarebytes

Before sending the query, Tony demonstrates a small time-saving trick he has configured himself.

Rather than typing @Malwarebytes manually each time, he has created an iPhone text replacement. Typing MW followed by a space automatically expands to @Malwarebytes.

This replacement is Tony's personal keyboard configuration, not a built-in Malwarebytes feature.

Sending the message for analysis

In the ChatGPT edit field, Tony enters @Malwarebytes, pastes the suspicious message, and adds the question ¿esta reportado?.

He activates Send. ChatGPT reports that it is preparing the app tools and then returns the Malwarebytes analysis.

What the check reports

The response identifies the text as an example of the familiar scam in which someone pretends to be a child or relative whose phone has supposedly broken or been lost and asks the recipient to continue the conversation using a new number.

The explanation says this kind of scam is commonly used to move the victim into a longer conversation that may later lead to requests for money connected with a fabricated emergency.

For the specific phone number, the service reports that no reputation information is registered in its database at that moment. Tony highlights an important point: the absence of reports does not automatically make a number safe. It may simply be a new or temporary number with no recorded history yet.

What this check is useful for

The demonstration does not present a single lookup result as an absolute guarantee. Its value is as an additional tool for investigating a message, number, email, or link before reacting too quickly.

In the example shown, the service can also recognise the wording of the message as a known scam pattern even though the number itself has no reputation data recorded.

Part two: WikiTrip and the history around you

Tony then changes subject completely and opens WikiTrip.

He explains that the app uses the user's location to find nearby points of interest and provide historical or cultural information about them. The idea is to walk or travel through an area while the app tells you stories about nearby buildings, monuments, and other places.

First example: Rascacielos de la Avenida 3 de Mayo

During the demonstration, WikiTrip announces a point of interest 0.2 km to the south: Rascacielos de la avenida 3 de mayo.

The app's narration describes it as an emblematic building in Santa Cruz de Tenerife, Canary Islands, Spain. It says that architect Carmelo Rodríguez Borrella designed it for the Cámara de la Propiedad Urbana and that it opened on May 3, 1974.

The narration also states that the building is 85 metres tall with 24 floors and that it remained the tallest building in the city until the Torres de Santa Cruz were built.

Second example: Castillo de Paso Alto

WikiTrip later detects another point 2.4 km to the northeast: Castillo de Paso Alto.

The app describes it as a fortification on the coast of Santa Cruz de Tenerife, between the Club Deportivo Militar de Paso Alto and the Escuela Técnica Superior de Náutica, Máquinas y Radioelectrónica Naval de la Universidad de La Laguna.

Its narration explains that the location had strategic value because currents and winds forced ships to sail close to the coast and that the fort played an important role in defending the city for centuries.

A different way to listen to your surroundings

Tony particularly likes the fact that WikiTrip does more than identify what is nearby. It adds historical and cultural context.

The app selects nearby points and reads information about them, turning an ordinary walk or journey into a different way of discovering the city.

Settings, the free version, and voices

Tony shows that WikiTrip has a Settings button.

He explains that paid options provide access to higher-quality synthetic voices, while the free version is already fully usable for the purpose demonstrated in the video.

Update note — September 8, 2026

The Malwarebytes integration with ChatGPT is still available and can still be invoked with @Malwarebytes to check suspicious messages, links, emails, screenshots, and phone numbers.

The discovery path has changed since the video was recorded. On July 9, 2026, ChatGPT moved the former Apps directory into the Plugin directory. Malwarebytes currently instructs users to go to Settings > Plugins > Browse plugins, search for Malwarebytes, install the plugin, and connect it. Existing connections were not affected by the directory migration.

Malwarebytes also now offers a standalone phone-number reputation checker and allows users to report suspicious numbers to help expand its threat-intelligence database.

WikiTrip remains available for iPhone. It continues to work as a location-based audio guide and still has a free mode. Its current App Store listing also offers AI voices through in-app purchases.

Closing

Tony closes by hoping that both ideas are useful: one as an extra layer of help when dealing with potentially fraudulent messages, and the other as a different way to discover the history of places around you.

He invites viewers to leave comments, like the video, and share it if they found it interesting.
