How to tell if someone has blocked you on WhatsApp

Accessible written version of a Canal TifloAcosta video. Watch the original video on YouTube.

A question with no direct answer

Tony addresses a very common question: how can you tell whether someone has blocked you on WhatsApp?

The first point is the most important. WhatsApp does not display a notice telling you that another person has blocked you. For privacy reasons, the app avoids giving a direct confirmation. What you can do instead is look at several indicators and consider them together.

No single sign is enough. A missing profile picture, an unavailable last seen time, or a delayed message can all have other explanations.

Why privacy settings can be misleading

Before checking a specific contact, Tony opens WhatsApp on his iPhone with VoiceOver and enters the settings area, labelled You in the interface shown in the video. He then opens Privacy.

The purpose is to show that many things people interpret as signs of blocking can also be caused by ordinary privacy settings.

Last seen and online status

The Last Seen and Online settings control who can access that information.

Tony reviews choices such as Everyone, My Contacts, My Contacts Except…, and Nobody. The online status can also be configured to follow the same rule as Last Seen.

The important point is that being unable to see someone's last seen time or online status does not by itself prove that you have been blocked. It may simply reflect that person's privacy settings.

Tony also points out the reciprocal nature of this setting: if you choose not to share your own last seen or online status, you also lose access to that information for other users.

Profile photo and About

The profile photo and About information also have privacy controls.

For that reason, if a contact's photo disappears or their About information is no longer visible, Tony warns against treating this as automatic proof of blocking. The contact may simply have restricted who can see those details.

Who can add you to groups

Tony also opens the privacy settings for Groups, where users decide who can add them directly to a group.

He reviews the available options and explains that when privacy settings prevent a direct group addition, WhatsApp can allow the group admin to send a private invitation instead.

He uses this section to demonstrate a way to stop people from adding you directly to groups: choose My Contacts Except… and exclude all contacts.

First check: profile photo, About, Last Seen, and Online

After reviewing privacy settings, Tony moves on to a specific contact.

If the contact's profile photo, About information, Last Seen, and Online status are all unavailable, that may raise suspicion. However, the privacy settings just demonstrated can produce all of those effects.

Tony therefore treats them as clues rather than proof.

Second check: send a message

Another check is to send the contact a message and observe its delivery status.

If the message remains on a single check mark, it has been sent from your phone but has not been shown as delivered to the recipient's device.

Tony explains that several situations can cause this and that it does not prove blocking on its own. The recipient may have no connection, their phone may be switched off, or another condition may be preventing delivery.

If the single check mark persists together with several other indicators, the suspicion becomes stronger.

Third check: WhatsApp calls

Tony suggests trying a WhatsApp voice or video call.

If the call does not connect, this can be added to the other indicators. It should still not be interpreted alone, because connectivity or device availability problems can produce similar behaviour.

The encryption check shown in the video

In the contact information screen, Tony opens the end-to-end encryption section, identified by the text explaining that messages and calls are end-to-end encrypted and can be tapped for verification.

In the video, he treats the result of this check as one more element to consider alongside the other signs.

This part now needs an important clarification, included in the update note below: encryption verification serves a different purpose and should not currently be treated as evidence that a contact has blocked you.

Trying to add the contact to a group

Another test is to try adding that person to a WhatsApp group.

If WhatsApp does not allow the contact to be added, Tony explains that this can be another possible indicator. However, the earlier privacy demonstration matters here too: group privacy settings can also prevent direct additions.

So this result cannot be interpreted in isolation.

The key is the combination of indicators

The video's conclusion is not that there is one infallible trick. It is the opposite.

If only the profile photo is missing, that is not enough. If only Last Seen is unavailable, that is not enough either. A message sitting on one check mark for a while is still not enough.

But if several signs occur together—contact information is no longer visible, messages consistently fail to deliver, calls do not connect, and you cannot directly add the person to a group—the possibility of being blocked becomes much stronger.

Even then, WhatsApp does not explicitly confirm the block to you.

If you really need to know

Tony reminds viewers that configuration changes, technical problems, or accidental situations can sometimes look like blocking.

If knowing for certain is genuinely important, he suggests using another communication route, such as a regular phone call or contacting the person directly.

Update note — September 8, 2026

The main point of the video remains valid: WhatsApp does not directly tell you that someone has blocked you, and several indicators should be considered together.

WhatsApp's current documentation lists possible signs including no longer seeing Last Seen or Online information, not seeing profile photo updates or statuses, messages remaining on a single check mark, calls not going through, and—in the case of a group admin—being unable to add that contact.

Encryption verification should not be used as a blocking test. Its purpose is to verify the security codes for an end-to-end encrypted chat. Those codes can change when someone reinstalls WhatsApp, changes phones, or adds or removes linked devices.

Someone removing you from their contacts does not by itself stop a message from being delivered. A persistent single check mark can result from causes such as the recipient's phone being off, lack of coverage or data, or blocking.

The group test still requires context. Being unable to add someone directly can be consistent with blocking, but it can also result from that person's group privacy settings.

Closing

Tony closes by stressing that these checks are useful for gathering clues rather than producing mathematical certainty. He invites viewers to leave comments, like the video, and share it if they found it useful.
