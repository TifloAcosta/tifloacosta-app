VoiceOver: Is That Voice New?

Tony Acosta welcomes viewers back to the TifloAcosta channel to continue exploring the iPhone with VoiceOver. This time, he looks at two different ways of expanding the range of voices available to the screen reader: Visionauta, a visual-assistance app that can also add voices to the system, and Runa TTS, a voice platform that was still in beta and not yet available on the App Store when the video was recorded.

Visionauta

Tony begins with Visionauta, spelling the name V-I-S-I-O-N-A-U-T-A. The app can be downloaded from the App Store, set to Spanish, and given the permissions it needs to operate. Although some of its features are similar to those found in other AI-based visual-assistance apps, it includes several useful touches of its own.

The main screen provides a number of controls. Turn On Flashlight can improve lighting in a dark environment. Magnifier is particularly useful for people with some usable vision because it enlarges the image captured by the camera. Activities keeps a history of actions performed with the app so they can be reviewed later.

More Options opens a menu containing the app’s own tutorial, Terms of Use, Privacy Policy, and an option to make a donation to support the developer.

Zoom In and Zoom Out controls are also available for adjusting magnification.

Visionauta provides two ways of using artificial intelligence. Online AI mode, described as the higher-quality option, requires an Internet connection and uses cloud services. Offline mode runs on the iPhone itself and can be used without an Internet connection for supported functions.

Real-Time Conversation lets the user point the camera at something and talk naturally with the AI about what is in front of them. Tony gives examples such as identifying a banknote or finding out what is inside a spice container. Read starts recognizing and reading text in front of the camera, such as a document, package, or sign. Type a Question makes it possible to enter a query with the keyboard when speaking aloud would not be appropriate. Voice Command can be used to ask the app to find an object, such as a phone, glass, or keys, while the camera searches for it.

Visionauta settings

In Settings, Tony reviews several sections of the app. Account provides options to sign in or create an account. Language lets the user choose the app language; in the demonstration it is set to Spanish. Localized Experience attempts to adapt some wording and responses to the selected location, which in Tony’s case is Spain.

Display settings include the app theme and an option to keep the screen awake. Tony uses the system theme and keeps the screen-awake option enabled while using Visionauta.

Under Camera, he selects the rear camera and generally recommends it because of the image quality it provides. Under Assistant, he keeps the high-quality online AI mode selected.

The Voice section contains several controls related to speech and audio. These include using the system speech engine, managing the installed native voice, adjusting speech rate, listening to a sample, selecting the voice used for real-time conversation — Juliana appears in the demonstration — adjusting real-time conversation volume, and enabling volume boost.

One point Tony highlights is that Visionauta’s voices are not necessarily confined to the app itself: voices can be added to the system, expanding the choices available to VoiceOver.

Runa TTS: additional voices for VoiceOver

In the second part of the video, Tony moves on to Runa TTS. At the time of recording, this voice platform was still in beta and had not yet been released on the App Store.

Its main attraction for VoiceOver users is the ability to add more voices to the system and use them as screen-reader voices. Tony switches voices through the VoiceOver rotor and moves around different iPhone screens so listeners can hear how each voice performs during normal navigation.

During the demonstration he tries several Spanish voices:

  • Manuel Enhanced.
  • Pilar Enhanced.
  • Alba Enhanced, with an Argentinian accent.
  • Ana Enhanced, with a neutral Latin American accent.
  • Violeta Enhanced, in enhanced quality.

Tony comments on the range of Spanish voices available in this early beta. The beta is free, but at the time of the recording there was no confirmed business model for the final release. It could eventually be free, a one-time purchase, subscription-based, or use a combination of those models.

These voices are not limited to VoiceOver. Once they are available to the system, they can also be used by reading apps that support iOS system voices, including Voice Dream Reader, Speech Central, and Bookshare Reader. This provides additional alternatives to the familiar voices many users already rely on when listening to books, documents, and other long-form content.
