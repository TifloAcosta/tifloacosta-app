zEdit: At Last, Editing Video and Audio with VoiceOver Stops Being a Torture

Canal TifloAcosta

Introduction

Tony Acosta introduces a zEdit app on Canal TifloAcosta that he had been looking for to work with videos, audio files, and photos in an accessible way with VoiceOver. He explains that, based on what he demonstrates in the video, zEdit lets him perform multimedia editing tasks with a level of independence he had not found in other tools he had used before.

Finding and opening zEdit in the App Store

To show how to find it, Tony opens the App Store and moves to the search field. He types “zEdit” and starts the search. In the results he locates the app in the photo and video category and opens it.

Main screen and settings

When zEdit opens, the main screen provides access to its media-editing functions. Before starting an edit, Tony enters Settings and goes through the available options.

The “Language” control lets the user choose the interface language, and Tony selects Spanish. The screen also includes toggles for “Show milliseconds”, “Optical feedback”, “Autoplay”, “Extended time control”, and “Dark interface”. During the demonstration, Tony explains that the option identified in the video as “Optical feedback” is enabled and produces a small vibration when certain actions are carried out, while “Autoplay” is also enabled.

For the default video format, the app offers MP4 and MOV. Tony selects MP4 because he considers it the more practical and commonly used format. For audio, the available choices shown include M4A and WAV; Tony leaves M4A selected because it produces a smaller file size.

The same Settings screen also contains “Clear cache”, a “Functions” section explaining the app’s tools, “Privacy Policy” and “Contact” options, and the app’s version and build information.

Extra tools: adding audio to a photo

Tony returns to the main screen and opens “Extra tools”. There he demonstrates “Add music/audio to photo”, which combines a still image with an audio file to create a video.

He activates “Add music/audio to photo” and then “Select photo”. The app offers a choice between the photo library and Files. Tony opens the photo library and chooses an image.

The app then asks for the sound file. Tony navigates through Files on the iPhone and selects an audio file. Once the picture and sound have been selected, the app sets the video duration according to the audio file and displays start and end controls that can be adjusted if necessary.

Tony activates “Apply”. The app displays “Creating video...” and, when the process finishes, reports “Video ready”. The result screen shows a preview and a file size of 8.9 MB. The system sharing and sending options are then available.

Editing tools shown in the video

Tony then goes through a number of editing tools available in the app.

“Cut and trim” is used to select and remove parts of the media along its timeline.

“Convert” can produce an audio-only file from a video.

“Add media” lets the user place additional clips before or after the current media, for example to join several videos or add another segment.

“Mute audio” can silence the whole track or a specific section.

“Adjust volume” can raise or lower the original audio level. Tony gives increases of 25% or 50% as examples.

“Add audio” lets the user place another sound track or background music over a video.

“Change speed” modifies playback speed.

Controls related to resolution and size also appear.

The app includes subtitle tools and automatic subtitles. Tony also explains the option to generate automatically translated subtitles in other languages while keeping the original audio.

The final tools he shows include “Noise reduction”, intended to clean background noise, and “Trim silence”, which detects and removes pauses or silent sections from the file.

Conclusion

Tony closes by emphasizing zEdit’s simplicity and the independence that, in his experience during the demonstration, it can offer a VoiceOver user when editing video and audio. He invites viewers to try the app, share the video, and leave comments, and then says goodbye until the next Canal TifloAcosta video.
