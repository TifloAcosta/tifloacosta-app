Create Accessibility Reports in Seconds from Your iPhone

Accessible written version of a Canal TifloAcosta video.

Introduction

Tony Acosta presents an iOS shortcut he created for Canal TifloAcosta to prepare accessibility reports through a guided process. The purpose is to collect the information needed when an accessibility barrier appears in an app or website, so the resulting report can later be shared with the appropriate person or organisation.

What the shortcut is designed to solve

Tony explains that he is not going to show how to build a shortcut from scratch in this video. Instead, he demonstrates a ready-made tool for a common situation: finding an accessibility problem and not being sure which details should be gathered to explain it clearly.

The shortcut asks a series of questions and uses the answers to create a structured report.

Opening the shortcut

Tony opens the Shortcuts app on the iPhone and finds “TifloAcosta informe de accesibilidad”.

He explains that it can be started directly from the Shortcuts app or invoked through Siri by saying its name.

When it runs, the screen reports “Shortcut running” and the questionnaire begins.

First question: app or service

The shortcut asks: “¿Qué aplicación o servicio quieres reportar?”.

A text field appears in editing mode. Tony types “WhatsApp” using the on-screen keyboard and activates “OK”.

Second question: what you were trying to do

The next question is: “¿Qué estabas intentando hacer?”.

Tony explains that the answer can be typed, dictated, or pasted from the clipboard. In the demonstration he opens the editing menu, chooses paste, and inserts:

“Ver mis nuevos mensajes”.

He then activates “OK”.

Third question: accessibility barrier

The shortcut asks: “¿Qué barrera de accesibilidad has encontrado?”.

Tony again pastes text from the clipboard:

“El foco de VoiceOver se vuelve loco, pierde el sentido y me desorienta”.

He then activates “OK”.

Fourth question: impact of the problem

The next question is: “¿Cómo afecta esto al uso de la aplicación o página?”.

Tony pastes:

“No tengo manera de saber dónde estoy y no puedo seguir el flujo de mensajes con orden”.

He confirms with “OK”.

Fifth question: device, system, and screen reader

Finally, the shortcut asks: “¿Qué dispositivo, sistema y lector de pantalla estás utilizando?”.

Tony explains that this information matters because an accessibility issue can depend on the particular combination of hardware, system version, and screen reader.

In the demonstration he enters:

“iPhone 15 Pro, iOS 26.5 con VoiceOver activado”.

He then activates “OK”.

Generated report and sharing options

After the final answer, the shortcut generates the report and automatically opens the iOS share sheet.

Options shown include AirDrop, Messages, Mail, and WhatsApp.

Tony explains that the report can be emailed to a support or accessibility address. As an example, he mentions accessibility@apple.com for an Apple-related case. He also says it can be sent through WhatsApp when the relevant contact is available, saved in Notes, or sent to oneself for review before submission.

How to obtain the shortcut

Tony says that the link for obtaining the shortcut will be available in the YouTube video notes and that it can also be requested through WhatsApp.

No complete shortcut URL is provided in the material used for this written adaptation, so this document does not reconstruct one.

The TifloAcosta community

Tony also mentions the Canal TifloAcosta WhatsApp community. He explains that materials are shared there, including guides for using WhatsApp on desktop in Windows and Mac, as well as courses on screen readers such as advanced JAWS for Windows, NVDA, and VoiceOver on Mac. He also says that some of these materials are being offered in English.

Conclusion

Tony encourages viewers to like the video, leave comments, and interact with the content. He closes by saying that more material will follow on Canal TifloAcosta.
