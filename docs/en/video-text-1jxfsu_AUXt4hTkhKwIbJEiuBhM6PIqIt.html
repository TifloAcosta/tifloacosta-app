iOS 26.4 without the new Siri, but with changes that actually matter

Accessible written version of a Canal TifloAcosta video. Watch the original video on YouTube.

An update without the expected Siri overhaul, but not an empty one

iOS 26.4 arrived without the major Siri overhaul many users had been waiting for. Tony starts with exactly that point: anyone installing the update expecting a completely new Siri would have to keep waiting.

That does not make iOS 26.4 uninteresting. The video focuses instead on quieter changes that can be far more useful in everyday life for people who use VoiceOver, a braille display, or visual accessibility settings.

Touch Curtain for braille display users

Inside Settings > Accessibility > VoiceOver > Braille, iOS 26.4 introduces a particularly useful feature: Touch Curtain.

When a braille display is connected, Touch Curtain prevents the iPhone touchscreen from receiving accidental input. This can be useful when the phone is in a pocket, being carried in one hand, or resting nearby while the user is controlling it from the braille display.

Touch Curtain should not be confused with VoiceOver's Screen Curtain. Screen Curtain visually turns off the display while leaving the iPhone active; Touch Curtain blocks touchscreen input while a braille display is being used.

New commands for moving between text fields

Another useful change can be found in Settings > Accessibility > VoiceOver > Commands > All Commands > Advanced Navigation.

iOS 26.4 adds two new actions:

  • Move to the previous text field.
  • Move to the next text field.

These commands let you jump directly from one text field to another without moving through every element in between. They can be especially convenient in forms, web pages, and apps with several edit boxes.

They are not necessarily assigned to a default gesture, so users can map them to a touch gesture, keyboard shortcut, or braille command that suits their workflow.

Reduce Bright Effects

Settings > Accessibility > Display & Text Size now includes Reduce Bright Effects.

The setting minimizes certain highlighting and flashing effects that can appear when interacting with onscreen elements such as buttons or the keyboard.

For someone with no useful vision who relies entirely on VoiceOver speech or braille, the change may have little practical effect. For low-vision users or people who are sensitive to bright visual effects, it may make the interface more comfortable.

Reduce Motion and Liquid Glass

Tony also reviews Settings > Accessibility > Motion > Reduce Motion.

iOS 26.4 improves this setting so that animations associated with Liquid Glass elements are reduced more consistently.

For VoiceOver users, reducing some animations can make navigation feel calmer, although it should not be presented as a universal fix for every VoiceOver focus problem.

Typing, braille, and small improvements that can matter

Tony comments on his experience with typing and VoiceOver after installing the update.

Apple officially states that iOS 26.4 improves keyboard accuracy when typing quickly. In braille, specialist user testing also suggested a partial improvement in the issue where letters could sometimes be dropped when typing with a braille display.

It is better to describe this as an improvement rather than a complete fix, because behaviour can vary depending on the app, input method, and hardware being used.

Notifications: still room for improvement

During the video, Tony notes that some notification behaviour still feels unsatisfactory. This should be understood as a practical observation from real-world use rather than as an issue Apple officially claimed to have fixed in iOS 26.4.

If a particular notification still behaves poorly with VoiceOver, users should not assume that the update necessarily resolved it.

The Ambient Music widget

When editing the Home Screen and adding a widget, iOS 26.4 offers an Ambient Music widget.

It provides direct access to music for sleep, relaxation, focus, or wellbeing without first navigating through the full Music app.

It is a small addition, but it fits the theme of Tony's video well: features that may not make the headlines but can save steps in everyday use.

What Apple officially included in iOS 26.4

Alongside the changes discussed in the video, Apple released iOS 26.4 with Apple Music enhancements, eight new emoji, accessibility improvements, bug fixes, and security updates.

Apple officially listed Reduce Bright Effects and improvements to Reduce Motion among the accessibility changes. It also added caption-related improvements and better keyboard accuracy when typing quickly.

Time context

This document adapts a video published on March 25, 2026 and describes iOS 26.4 as it existed at launch. Apple has since released later versions of iOS 26, so some bugs, behaviours, or menu details may have changed.

The reference to the missing Siri overhaul belongs to that moment in time. There is no need to speculate here about which later version would eventually deliver it.

Conclusion

iOS 26.4 was not the update that transformed Siri, but it did bring several worthwhile accessibility improvements: Touch Curtain for braille users, new commands for moving between text fields, fewer disruptive bright effects, more effective motion reduction, and modest progress in typing.

They are not spectacular changes, but some can remove small frustrations every day. For someone who depends on VoiceOver or a braille display, that kind of practical improvement can matter far more than a flashy feature that is rarely used.
