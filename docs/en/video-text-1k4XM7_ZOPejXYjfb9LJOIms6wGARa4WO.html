VoiceOver touch gestures on iOS: navigating and controlling iPhone without sight

This tutorial adapts the Canal TifloAcosta video “Gestos de Voice Over”. The gestures and their functions are described as they are explained and demonstrated in the original recording, made with the version of iOS available at that time. Some gestures or behaviours may have changed in later versions of the operating system.

Watch the original “Gestos de Voice Over” video on YouTube.

Introduction to VoiceOver and the basic concepts

VoiceOver is the screen reader built into iOS devices such as iPhone. It is designed to let blind and low-vision people use the device through specific gestures on the touchscreen.

When VoiceOver is turned on, the usual way of interacting with iPhone changes. Touching an item does not immediately activate it. Instead, the item is selected and VoiceOver announces its name or content. To activate the selected item, perform a one-finger double tap anywhere on the screen.

Exploring the screen

Tony explains two main ways to move around the interface with VoiceOver.

Direct touch exploration

Move one finger across the screen without lifting it. As your finger moves, VoiceOver announces the items underneath it.

Exploring with swipes

  • One-finger swipe right: move VoiceOver focus to the next item.
  • One-finger swipe left: move VoiceOver focus to the previous item.

In the video, Tony demonstrates this by moving through the icons on the Home Screen and showing how VoiceOver focus travels linearly from one app to the next.

Activating items and basic interactions

Once VoiceOver focus is on the required item, different gestures can be used to perform actions.

  • One-finger double tap: open an app or activate the button or control currently focused.
  • One-finger double tap and hold: acts as a long press. In the video, examples include entering Home Screen editing mode, moving apps, and opening contextual shortcut menus.
  • One-finger triple tap: performs a secondary action or a prolonged action depending on the context of the app.

Moving through pages and lists with three fingers

To move through screens, documents, or lists, Tony explains the use of three fingers at the same time.

  • Three-finger swipe left: move to the next page, for example on the Home Screen.
  • Three-finger swipe right: return to the previous page.
  • Three-finger swipe up: move the list or page downward.
  • Three-finger swipe down: move the list or page upward.

During the demonstration, VoiceOver announces messages such as “Page 2 of 3” and “Page 1 of 3”, allowing the user to know their current position.

Controlling VoiceOver reading

VoiceOver also has specific gestures for controlling continuous reading.

  • Two-finger tap: pause or resume VoiceOver speech at the current point.
  • Two-finger swipe up: read the entire screen from the beginning.
  • Two-finger swipe down: start continuous reading from the item that is currently selected.

The Magic Tap

The two-finger double tap, known as the Magic Tap, performs the main action for the current context.

The video gives several examples:

  • When a phone call comes in, it answers the call; during the call, performing it again ends the call.
  • When music or audio is playing, it pauses or resumes playback.
  • In Camera, it takes a photo.

Tony demonstrates the gesture by playing audio and using the two-finger double tap to stop and restart playback.

The Escape or scrub gesture

The Escape gesture is performed by quickly moving two fingers back and forth across the screen, approximately drawing the shape of a letter “Z” or using a scrubbing motion.

It can be used to go back, close a floating menu, cancel a pop-up window, or leave the current screen when the app supports that action.

The VoiceOver rotor

The rotor is a virtual tool that changes the way VoiceOver reads text or moves around a screen.

To turn it, place two fingers on the screen — for example your index finger and thumb — and rotate them as though you were turning an invisible dial. VoiceOver announces the available rotor options as you move.

Options can include Headings, Links, Characters, Words, Speaking Rate, Volume, and Controls, among others.

Once an option is selected, swipe one finger up or down to use it. For example, when Words is selected, vertical swipes move through text one word at a time.

Privacy and VoiceOver status

Tony explains several three- and four-finger gestures that control additional screen-reader functions.

  • Three-finger triple tap: turn Screen Curtain on or off. When it is enabled, the display becomes completely black while VoiceOver continues to work. Tony explains that it can be useful for saving battery and for privacy in public places.
  • Three-finger double tap: mute or unmute VoiceOver speech.
  • Three-finger quadruple tap: copy the last text spoken by VoiceOver to the clipboard.
  • Four-finger tap near the top of the screen: move VoiceOver focus to the first visible item.
  • Four-finger tap near the bottom of the screen: move focus to the last visible item.

Practice is part of learning

Tony finishes by reminding viewers that fluency with VoiceOver comes through practice. At first it can be difficult to coordinate several fingers or remember which gesture performs each action, but with daily use the movements become a natural part of controlling the iPhone.

The goal is not to memorise everything at once. It is better to gradually learn the gestures used most often and expand that set according to each person's needs.
