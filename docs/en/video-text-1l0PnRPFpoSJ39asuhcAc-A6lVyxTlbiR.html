What If Speaking Another Language Stopped Being a Barrier?

Introduction

Tony Acosta presents a feature of Google Translate on Canal TifloAcosta that can provide real-time translation and interpretation directly on a phone. He explains that the app is available on both iPhone and Android, and presents it as a practical way to reduce the barrier created by not understanding the language of a conversation, conference, or audiovisual programme.

Installing and opening the app

Tony explains that the app he is using is Google Translate and that it can be obtained from the appropriate app store for the device.

When the app opens, he shows that it is possible to sign in with a Gmail account. He recommends doing so to make use of the options associated with the account, although he also makes clear that the app can be used without signing in.

Main screen and basic functions

Tony explores the main Google Translate screen with VoiceOver.

He finds the “Enter text” field, which is used to type or paste content for translation.

He also shows the “Translate handwriting” button. During the explanation, he says that this function can be used to work with written text and obtain a translation. He adds, as an example, that if the text is in Spanish, VoiceOver can use text recognition to read what appears on screen.

In the language controls, the source language is set to English and the target language to Spanish. Between the two controls is “Swap languages”, which reverses the direction of translation.

Real-time instant translation

Tony opens the option identified as “Use AI-powered instant translation”. This brings up a screen containing the settings for real-time interpretation.

Under “Their language” he selects English, while “Your language” remains Spanish. The app also provides a control for swapping the two languages.

In the playback options, Tony shows that translated speech can be played through connected headphones, or through the device speakers when headphones are not connected.

A “Text only” option is also available. Tony explains that in this mode the app does not speak the translation aloud, allowing the user to read the translated text on screen with VoiceOver.

The “Start” button activates microphone listening so that the app can begin interpreting audio in the selected source language.

First demonstration: an English phrase

Tony activates “Start” and carries out a short test using the English phrase “Hello How are you”.

The app immediately provides the Spanish translation: “Hola ¿cómo estás?”.

This first example demonstrates the basic behaviour of the interpretation function while the phone listens to a phrase in the configured source language.

Second demonstration: audio from a video

Tony then plays a video in English to extend the test.

While the video is playing, Google Translate listens to the audio and provides a real-time interpretation into Spanish. In the demonstration, part of a speech about the arrival of a revolutionary Apple product is translated.

Tony uses this example to show that the function is not limited to a face-to-face conversation and can also be used to follow spoken material being played in another language.

Uses Tony highlights

Based on the demonstrations, Tony says that the feature can be useful for following conferences, presentations, or videos in languages the user does not understand. He gives French, German, and Japanese as examples.

He also stresses that, as shown in the video, no dedicated translation hardware is required and the method does not depend on features such as Apple Intelligence: the tool being used is Google Translate installed on the phone.

Conclusion

Tony closes by encouraging viewers to use the feature to reduce language barriers. He asks the audience to like the video, share it, and subscribe to Canal TifloAcosta. During the closing remarks he mentions that the channel has already passed 4,000 subscribers and ends by sending viewers a hug.
