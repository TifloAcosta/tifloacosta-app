Vox Libri: a book reader genuinely designed with blind users in mind

Accessible written version of a Canal TifloAcosta video. Watch the original video on YouTube.

Vox Libri on the App Store.

A reading app with accessibility at the centre

Tony begins 2026 by introducing Vox Libri, an app created by Jonathan Chacón Barbero for reading books and documents on screen, with a braille display, or through text-to-speech.

Tony particularly highlights that Jonathan is blind and that the app was conceived from the outset around accessibility and practical usability for people who rely on assistive technology.

The demonstration is carried out on an iPhone with VoiceOver.

Finding Vox Libri on the App Store

Tony opens the App Store and searches for VOX LIBRI, as two separate words, until he finds Vox Libri: Books.

At the time of recording, the app cost €5.99 in Spain and was sold as a one-time purchase rather than a subscription.

Because Tony had already purchased it, his device shows the Open button.

Information shown on the App Store page

Tony moves through the App Store listing and checks several details:

  • Developer: Jonathan Chacón Barbero.
  • Size shown at the time: 12.4 MB.
  • Compatibility shown then: iPhone and iPad.
  • Languages: Spanish plus one additional language.

The description presents Vox Libri as a flexible and accessible reading environment that can be used on screen, through braille, or with built-in speech.

Formats and reading methods

The listing highlights read-aloud speech, braille-display support, and a customizable reading screen with options such as font size, colours, spacing, and themes.

At the time, supported formats included TXT, PDF, DOCX, RTF, HTML, Markdown, and EPUB.

Navigation through chapters and headings is also highlighted.

Accessibility declared on the App Store

Tony pays particular attention to the Accessibility section.

The developer declares support for several system accessibility features, including:

  • VoiceOver.
  • Voice Control.
  • Larger Text.
  • Dark Interface.
  • Differentiate Without Color Alone.
  • Sufficient Contrast.
  • Reduced Motion.

Tony values the fact that accessibility is presented as a core part of the app rather than as a secondary add-on.

Privacy

In the privacy section, Tony checks that the developer declares that the app does not collect data.

The Vox Libri library

Tony activates Open and enters the main screen.

The library provides several controls for managing content:

  • Add Book, for importing new material.
  • Filters, for organizing or narrowing the library.
  • Search, for finding titles.
  • Settings, for the app's general configuration.

The available books and documents appear below the Library heading.

The manual and a TifloAcosta document

The library includes the Vox Libri Manual, together with reading-progress information.

It also contains an imported document titled TifloAcosta Píldoras Tecnológicas 29 de diciembre de 2025, which Tony uses for the demonstration.

Opening a document and starting to read

Tony opens the TifloAcosta document. The reading screen contains controls for searching, speech, and playback.

The Search button can locate specific words inside the open document. Tony gives the example of searching for “WhatsApp” in a long catalogue or text.

Volume, speed, and sleep timer

The screen includes a Volume control, set to 100% during the demonstration, and a Speed control, set to 50%.

There is also a Sleep Timer that can stop reading automatically after a selected amount of time.

Reading-progress information

Vox Libri provides time information about playback. In Tony's example, VoiceOver announces values such as 2 minutes elapsed, 5 minutes 56 seconds remaining, and a total reading time of approximately 7 minutes.

This makes it possible to know how far through the document the reader has progressed and roughly how much remains.

Choosing how to move through the document

An important control is Navigation.

Tony explains that it changes the unit used by the previous and next controls. Options shown include navigation by paragraph, page, or heading.

Once a unit is selected, Back and Forward move to the previous or next item of that type.

For example, when heading navigation is selected, the controls can jump between headings without reading all the text in between.

Play and pause

The Play button begins continuous text-to-speech reading, and the same control can be used to pause it.

Tony starts playback and lets Vox Libri read part of the TifloAcosta document, demonstrating the continuous reading experience.

A very positive assessment

Tony thanks Jonathan Chacón for developing an app designed around the reading needs of blind people, whether they prefer speech or braille.

He also appreciates the one-time purchase model instead of a recurring subscription.

One improvement Tony would like to see

As a possible future improvement, Tony says he would like more voice options, including personalized voices or additional speech platforms.

That comment is particularly interesting in hindsight because Vox Libri later added support for Personal Voice. The development is described in the update note below.

Update note — September 8, 2026

Vox Libri is still available. The Spanish App Store currently lists it at €5.99. It remains a paid app rather than a subscription service.

The app has expanded considerably since January. The current version is 1.19, released in early September 2026. Vox Libri is now available not only on iPhone and iPad, but also on Mac and Apple Watch.

Languages. The current listing includes Spanish, English, Italian, French, and Greek.

Size. The current Spanish listing shows a size of approximately 39.7 MB, so the 12.4 MB figure in the video belongs to a much earlier version.

Personal Voice. One of Tony's requested improvements arrived shortly afterwards. Since version 1.3, released in February 2026, Vox Libri can use Personal Voice for reading.

More formats. Several formats have been added to the original set during 2026, including ODT, Word 97/2003 DOC, MOBI, AZW3, KPF, HTMLZ, RTFD, FB2, and LaTeX.

Sync across devices. Vox Libri added iCloud library syncing, followed by an Apple Watch app and later a macOS version.

Siri and Shortcuts. Supported functions can now be controlled through Siri and the Shortcuts app.

VoiceOver. Updates have continued to include VoiceOver-specific improvements, including continuous-page reading, braille improvements, an optional setting to update reading progress by following VoiceOver focus, and fixes involving Magic Tap, navigation, library use, and pagination.

Reading customization. The reading screen and hands-free mode now offer more customization options. Background music while reading has also been introduced and remains identified as a beta feature.

Version 1.19. The latest update improves file handling in both local and cloud libraries and improves headphone handling when more than two different headphones are used during the same reading session.

Current accessibility and privacy declarations. The App Store listing still declares support for VoiceOver, Voice Control, Larger Text, Dark Interface, Differentiate Without Color Alone, Sufficient Contrast, and Reduced Motion. The developer also continues to state that the app does not collect data.

Conclusion

Tony's demonstration presents Vox Libri as a reading app whose main difference is not simply opening many file formats, but providing controls designed from the beginning for listening, braille reading, and efficient navigation through long documents.

The app's later development has kept that accessibility focus while substantially expanding the supported devices, formats, and reading options.

Closing

Tony finishes by thanking Jonathan Chacón, encourages viewers to like the video, and asks them to share it so more people can discover Vox Libri.
