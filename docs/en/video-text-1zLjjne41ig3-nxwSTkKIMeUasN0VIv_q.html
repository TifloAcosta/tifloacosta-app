SonarPad: The Accessible App That Wants to Do Everything

Canal TifloAcosta

Introduction

Tony Acosta introduces SonarPad for iOS on Canal TifloAcosta. He explains that it was developed by an Italian programmer named Ambrogio and, with some humour, describes it as a kind of “multi-processor” tool, comparing it to a food processor because of the number of different functions it brings together in one place.

Throughout the video he shows how to find the app in the App Store, reviews information about the project, explores the text-to-speech settings, and demonstrates several built-in utilities, using VoiceOver throughout to assess accessibility.

Finding SonarPad in the App Store

Tony opens the App Store from the app switcher and moves to the search field. He types “sonarpad”.

In the results, VoiceOver announces the app as “sonarpad Leggi ascolta organiza button”. Since it is already installed on the iPhone, the available control says “Open”. Tony also notes that some parts of the interface still contain Italian terms, which he associates with the developer’s origin.

About SonarPad

After opening the app, Tony explores the main screen and finds the “About” button.

This section shows version “0.3.1 build 20”. Tony points out that this is still an early 0.x release. He explains that the app is free and that a donation button is available.

There is also a “What’s New” button. The information shown describes SonarPad as an app designed to work accessibly with VoiceOver for blind and visually impaired users. Functions mentioned include listening to news, searching for and subscribing to podcasts, importing Wikipedia articles, and adding, saving, and editing documents in the library.

The screen also includes a button to “Visit the Sonarpad website” and displays the developer’s name, Ambrogio. Tony publicly thanks him for his work.

He then activates “Back” to return to the main menu.

Settings and text-to-speech engine

Tony opens “Settings” and moves through the available options.

The app language is set to Spanish. The theme is set to “system”, so it follows the device’s general appearance setting. The weather temperature unit is set to Celsius.

Under the TTS reading engine, two alternatives are shown: “HTTS high quality online” and “System voices voiceover/google”. Tony keeps the high-quality online voice option selected.

When choosing the TTS language, Tony finds an unusual label. While looking for Spanish from Spain, VoiceOver announces “Spanish Colombia es es”. Tony explains that the label is confusing, but that the option ending in “es es” gives access to the voices for Spain.

Within that selection, three neural voices are offered: “Shimena neural”, “Álvaro neural”, and “Elvira neural”. Tony leaves “Shimena neural” selected.

Reading speed and pitch are controlled with two adjustable settings, both shown initially at 1.0x. A “Test voice” button is also available for hearing a sample of the configured synthesizer.

The “Automatic resume” toggle is enabled. Tony explains that this lets the app continue documents, podcasts, or other media from the point where reading or playback had previously stopped.

To confirm the changes, he activates “Save settings”, and VoiceOver reports “Settings saved”.

Pronunciation dictionary

SonarPad includes a pronunciation dictionary for customising how specific words are spoken.

Tony explains that this can be used to correct names or expressions that the TTS engine does not pronounce as intended. As an example, he shows that a form interpreted incorrectly by the voice, such as “onolulu”, can be associated with “honolulu”, so the engine uses the corrected pronunciation whenever that term appears in documents or articles.

Weather information

Tony returns to the utilities menu and opens “Weather”.

The “My city” field contains “Santa Cruz de Tenerife”. After activating “Search”, the app displays information for “today”.

The values shown during the demonstration are:

Current condition: light drizzle. Current temperature: 23.5 ºC. Maximum temperature: 24.7 ºC. Minimum temperature: 20.3 ºC. Chance of precipitation: 84%. Precipitation: 1.1 mm. Wind: 20.8 km/h. Relative humidity: 71%.

Tony comments that, as with other weather apps, the figures may not always match the exact real-world conditions at a particular moment. Even so, he highlights the amount of accessible information the app provides on a single screen.

He then goes back.

Route calculation

The next utility Tony demonstrates is “Routes”, intended for geographic and mobility-related queries.

The screen includes a “From” field for the starting point and a “To” field for the destination. In the example, Cádiz is shown as the origin and Bilbao as the destination. Country selection is also available and is set to “Spain”.

Transport options include choices such as “by car” and “by wheelchair”.

Tony comments on the “by wheelchair” option and explains that it is intended to provide information about routes adapted for people with reduced mobility, including towns or intermediate points along the journey.

After examining the interface, he returns to the previous menu.

Conclusion

Tony closes by noting that SonarPad is still at an early stage of development and that some translation or labelling details could be improved. Even so, he considers it a very complete accessible app with many functions gathered in one place and encourages viewers to download and try it.

He also invites viewers to leave comments with their opinions about the features shown or suggestions that might be useful to the developer. Finally, he asks for the usual “like”, encourages people to share the video, and says goodbye until the next Canal TifloAcosta video.
